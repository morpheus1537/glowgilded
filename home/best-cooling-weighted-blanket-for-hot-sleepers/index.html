What makes a weighted blanket "cooling"?

There is no single standard, so read listings carefully. These are the features that matter most for hot sleepers:

  1. Fabric: Cotton and breathable microfiber generally feel less stuffy than plush minky or sherpa.
  2. Glass beads: All four picks here use glass beads, which are smaller and denser than plastic pellets, so the blanket can be thinner for the same weight.
  3. Pocket size: Small stitched compartments, such as ZonLi's 5x5-inch squares, help keep weight evenly spread.
  4. Weight: Choosing the lighter end of the suggested range can feel cooler and easier to adjust to.

Buying tips for hot sleepers

  • Go lighter, not heavier: If you are between sizes, the lighter weight usually feels cooler and is easier to get used to.
  • Skip the extra duvet: Use the weighted blanket on top of a flat sheet instead of over a thick comforter.
  • Check the fabric first: Cotton or breathable microfiber is a better start than sherpa or faux fur.
  • Plan for washing: Big, heavy blankets can strain home washers; a removable cover makes cleaning easier.
  • Mind your bedroom humidity: In dry winter rooms a humidifier helps comfort; our guide to the best humidifiers for the bedroom covers quiet options.

Frequently asked questions

Do weighted blankets make you hot at night?

They can, because a heavy blanket traps more body heat than a light comforter. Hot sleepers usually do better with breathable fabrics such as cotton or cooling microfiber, a lighter weight, and a blanket that sits on top of the mattress without a heavy duvet layered over it.

How heavy should a weighted blanket be?

Most brands in this guide suggest about 8 to 12 percent of your body weight. Bare Home uses 10 percent as its rule, so a 150-pound adult would choose a 15-pound blanket.

Can two people share one weighted blanket?

Most weighted blankets are sized and weighted for one person, so each sleeper usually does best with their own. The brands in this guide base their weight charts on a single person's body weight.

Why is my weighted blanket smaller than my bed?

That is intentional. ZonLi, Topcee and Bare Home all note that weighted blankets are smaller than regular comforters so the weight rests on you instead of hanging off the sides of the mattress.

Can you machine wash a weighted blanket?

It depends on the blanket and your washer. Bare Home and Mr. Sandman list machine washing, while ZonLi says its blanket may exceed household washer capacity and suggests a removable duvet cover instead.
