How to choose thermal blackout curtains

  1. Construction: Triple-weave fabric blocks most light on its own. A separate blackout lining usually blocks more and adds a little extra body.
  2. Light blocking: Look at the listed percentage. Triple-weave panels are often listed at 85% to 99%; lined panels at 100%.
  3. Size: Measure the window width and height. Two panels should add up to about twice the window width for a full look.
  4. Length: Floor-length panels cover more cold surface. Shorter panels suit windows over radiators or furniture.
  5. Hardware: Grommets with a 1.6-inch inner diameter fit most standard rods and slide easily.
  6. Care: Machine-washable panels make winter dust and pet hair easy to deal with.

Tips for a warmer window this winter

  • Hang the rod high and wide: Mount the rod a few inches above the frame and past each side so the curtains overlap the wall and cover gaps.
  • Close them at dusk: Curtains help most when they are drawn as soon as the sun goes down.
  • Open them on sunny days: South-facing windows can let in free warmth during the day.
  • Layer if needed: A sheer panel in front keeps daytime privacy without losing the blackout layer at night.
  • Seal drafts too: Curtains help, but weatherstripping and draft stoppers deal with air leaks at the source.
  • Warm the bed: Pair dark curtains with a warm bed. See our best heated mattress pads for cold sleepers.
  • Wake up gently: Blackout rooms stay dark in the morning. A sunrise alarm clock can help you get up on time.

Frequently asked questions

Do thermal blackout curtains really keep a room warmer in winter?

They can help. Thick, tightly woven or lined curtains add a layer between the room and a cold window pane, and makers like NICETOWN and ChrisDowa list them as insulating against winter chill and heat loss. Results depend on your windows, how well the curtains cover the frame and whether you keep them closed at night.

What is the difference between blackout and 100% blackout curtains?

Many triple-weave blackout curtains are listed as blocking 85% to 99% of light, with darker colors blocking more. Curtains with a separate blackout lining, like the H.VERSAILTEX and MIULEE linen panels, are listed as blocking 100% of light.

What size thermal curtains do I need?

For good coverage, choose panels whose combined width is about two times the width of your window and a length that reaches at least the sill, or the floor for the most draft protection. Two 52-inch panels cover a window up to roughly 52 inches wide with fullness.

Should thermal curtains touch the floor?

Floor-length curtains cover more of the cold wall and window frame, which helps if drafts are your main concern. Shorter 63-inch panels suit windows above radiators, beds or desks where long curtains would get in the way.

Can I wash blackout curtains?

All five pairs here are listed as machine washable. Most makers recommend cold or cool water, a gentle cycle and low heat, so check the care label before washing.
