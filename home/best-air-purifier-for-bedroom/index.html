How to choose an air purifier for your bedroom

  1. CADR (clean air delivery rate): The higher the CADR, the faster it cleans. Look for an AHAM-verified number.
  2. Room size: Check the square footage the maker lists for one or more air changes per hour.
  3. Sleep mode noise: Look for a listed decibel figure for the lowest setting.
  4. Light control: A display you can turn off matters in a dark bedroom.
  5. Filter type: HEPA-grade filters capture fine particles; carbon layers help with odors.
  6. Footprint: Small rooms need a purifier that fits by the bed or on a dresser.

Bedroom air purifier buying tips

  • Size up, then turn down: A purifier rated for a bigger room can run on a quieter low setting and still clean well.
  • Run it all the time: Purifiers work best running continuously with doors and windows closed.
  • Give it space: Keep a foot or two of clearance around the intake and outlet.
  • Budget for filters: Check replacement filter availability before you buy, and change them when the indicator tells you.
  • Pair it with the right humidity: Dry winter air can feel harsh. A humidifier helps, and our cool mist vs warm mist guide explains which type suits heated apartments.
  • Mind the temperature: Purifiers move air but do not heat it; see how to keep a cold bedroom warm for winter comfort.

Air purifiers and allergies

Air purifiers can reduce airborne dust, pollen and pet dander, but they do not remove dust that has settled on bedding, carpets and furniture. Wash sheets weekly in hot water, vacuum regularly and keep pets off the bed if allergies are a problem. If you have asthma or severe allergies, talk to your doctor about what will help most.

Frequently asked questions

What size air purifier do I need for a bedroom?

Match the purifier's CADR to the room. A common rule of thumb is a smoke CADR of at least two-thirds of the room's square footage, so a 150 sq ft bedroom needs roughly 100 CFM or more. The Levoit Core 300-P lists a 143 CFM smoke CADR.

Is it OK to sleep with an air purifier on all night?

Yes. Bedroom purifiers are designed to run overnight, and most have a sleep mode that lowers the fan speed and dims the lights. Keep it a few feet from the bed and away from curtains so air can flow freely.

Are air purifiers loud at night?

Good bedroom models are quiet in sleep mode. Levoit lists 24dB for the Core 300-P in sleep mode, the Vital 200S-P is listed at 27dB and Blueair lists the Blue Pure 511i Max as quiet as 19 dB.

Where should I put an air purifier in my bedroom?

Place it on the floor or a stable surface with a foot or two of clearance on all sides, ideally within 6-10 feet of where you sleep. Avoid tucking it into a corner or behind furniture.

How often do bedroom air purifier filters need replacing?

It depends on the model and how often you run it, so follow the filter indicator. Levoit recommends using its official replacement filters, and Blueair's app tracks filter life based on usage and pollution levels.
