What to look for in a sunrise alarm clock

  1. Sunrise length: Most clocks let you choose how long the light takes to reach full brightness. A longer sunrise is gentler; a shorter one suits people who wake quickly.
  2. Light coverage: A bigger glowing surface lights more of the room, which matters when it is fully dark outside.
  3. Backup sound: Light alone may not wake everyone. Natural sounds or FM radio at alarm time act as a safety net.
  4. Display dimming: A bright clock face can keep light sleepers awake. Look for a display you can dim or switch off.
  5. Bedtime features: Sleep sounds, a sunset mode or a sleep timer make the clock useful at night too.

Tips for getting the most from a wake-up light

  • Place it close: Put the clock on your nightstand, roughly at head height, so the light reaches your eyes. A clock across the room has much less effect.
  • Start with 30 minutes: Begin with a 20 to 30 minute sunrise, then lengthen or shorten it depending on how you wake.
  • Keep a backup alarm: For the first week, keep your phone alarm set a few minutes later, just in case.
  • Block streetlight at night: Blackout curtains keep the room dark overnight so the sunrise stands out in the morning. Open them once you are up.
  • Dim the display: Turn the clock face down or off at night if you are sensitive to light.
  • Add moisture: Winter heating dries out bedroom air. A quiet bedroom humidifier can make dark mornings more comfortable.

Frequently asked questions

Do sunrise alarm clocks really work in winter?

A sunrise alarm clock slowly brightens before your alarm time so you are not woken in total darkness, which many people find gentler on dark winter mornings. Results vary by person, so pair it with a consistent bedtime and an audible backup alarm until you know how you respond.

How long should the sunrise last on a wake-up light?

Most people start with 20 to 30 minutes and adjust from there. The JALL clocks in this guide let you choose 10 to 60 minutes, and the REACHER clock offers 5 to 60 minutes.

Are sunrise alarm clocks good for heavy sleepers?

They can help, but light alone may not wake a very heavy sleeper. Choose a model that also plays a sound or the radio at alarm time, such as the JALL full-screen clock, which is marketed for heavy sleepers.

Do I need an app or Wi-Fi for a sunrise alarm clock?

No. All four clocks here are set with buttons on the device. The original JALL syncs its time from your phone over Bluetooth, but none of them requires Wi-Fi or a subscription.

Can a sunrise alarm clock help me fall asleep too?

Several models include a sunset or dimming mode and sleep sounds with a timer. The Philips SmartSleep simulates a sunset, while the JALL and REACHER clocks pair sleep sounds with an automatic shut-off timer.
