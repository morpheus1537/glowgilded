Timer vs. sensor: which should you choose?

  • Fixed timer: Best if you want predictable hours, for example 5 PM to 11 PM every night. You simply switch the candles on at the right time once, and they repeat daily.
  • Dusk sensor with shut-off: Best for hands-off decorating. The candles follow sunset, which moves earlier through December, and still switch off after a set time.
  • Dusk-to-dawn sensor: Glows all night, which looks great for late arrivals but uses more battery power.
  • Remote timer: Best for clusters of candles on a mantel or table where you want to change settings without picking each one up.

Buying tips

  • Count your windows first: Most sets come in 4 or 6. Count the windows that face the street before buying so every window matches.
  • Measure the sill: Check depth for the base and the height from sill to the bottom of any blinds or valance.
  • Buy batteries in bulk: None of these sets include batteries. Rechargeable AA or AAA cells can save money over a long season, but check the manufacturer's guidance on battery type.
  • Turn them on at the same time: For timer models, switch all candles on at the time you want them to light each evening so they stay in sync.
  • Keep curtains clear: Even without a flame, keep candles stable and away from curtains that could knock them off the sill.

Frequently asked questions

How do battery operated window candles with a timer work?

Most start their timer cycle when you switch them on. The 612 Vermont timer candles, for example, stay on for 6 hours and off for 18 hours, then repeat every day, so switch them on at the time you want them to light each evening.

What is the difference between a timer and a dusk-to-dawn sensor?

A timer runs on a fixed schedule from the moment you turn it on. A light sensor turns the candle on when it gets dark. The 612 Vermont sensor model combines both, staying on for 8 hours after dark or until it senses light, whichever comes first.

How long do the batteries last in window candles?

It depends on the model and settings. The 612 Vermont timer candles list about 60 days on 2 AA batteries, and the Xodus listing says up to 40 days on 2 AA alkaline batteries. Batteries are not included with either.

Are flameless window candles safe to leave on?

They use LED lights instead of a flame, so there is no wax, open fire or hot glass. Still, follow each manufacturer's directions and keep them clear of curtains that could knock them over.

Why do people put candles in the window at Christmas?

Window candles are an old tradition, often described as a sign of welcome to travelers and family coming home for the holidays. Today most people use LED versions for the same warm look without the fire risk.
