How to decorate a bedroom for Christmas

Layer three kinds of light

The coziest bedrooms mix a big soft glow, a few small points of light and darkness at bedtime. Curtain lights on the window give the background glow, the birch trees add small points of sparkle at eye level, and both have timers so the room goes dark when you sleep. Keep everything warm white; cool white and multicolor lights look busy in a small room.

Pick one color story

Bedrooms feel calmer with fewer colors. A cream, red and green plaid throw works with natural wood and white bedding; if your room leans gray, add a white faux fur or knit throw instead and let the lights carry the holiday feeling. Two or three accents in the same palette, such as a pillow cover and a small wreath, are plenty.

Keep the nightstand usable

A small tree should not crowd out your lamp, phone and water glass. If space is tight, put the birch trees on the dresser or a windowsill instead, and use their timer so you never need to reach over in the dark. For more small-space ideas, read how to keep a cold bedroom warm.

Think about guests

The same three pieces turn a spare room into a welcoming guest room for the holidays. Pair them with the basics from our guest room essentials for holiday guests list, and leave the curtain-light remote somewhere obvious so guests can dim it.

Safety and setup tips

  • Use the timers. Lights that switch off on their own are safer and easier on batteries.
  • Never cover light strings with heavy fabric or bunch them under bedding.
  • Hang curtain lights from a rod rather than tape on painted walls, which can peel paint when removed.
  • Keep fringe throws and small trees out of reach of puppies, kittens and toddlers who like to chew.

How can I make my bedroom feel like Christmas?

Start with warm white light, add one or two pieces of seasonal texture, and keep the rest simple. Fairy curtain lights behind a sheer curtain, a small lighted tree on the nightstand and a plaid throw on the bed make a bedroom feel festive without clutter or a full-size tree.
