How to stop a shower curtain from billowing

  1. Choose weight: Heavier liners resist air movement better than thin, cheap ones. Look for terms like heavy duty, 8G or 10G.
  2. Add weight at the hem: Weighted stones, a lead-wire hem or magnets keep the bottom edge down.
  3. Use every hook: Hanging all 12 hooks gives even tension across the rod.
  4. Tuck the hem: Tucking the bottom inch or two inside the tub rim keeps the liner from swinging out.
  5. Consider a curved rod: A curved rod adds elbow room so the liner has space before it reaches you.

Buying tips

  • Check your tub material: Magnets only work on steel or cast iron tubs. For acrylic or fiberglass, choose a weighted hem or stones.
  • Size first: 72 x 72 inches fits most standard tubs. Measure if you have a wide or extra-tall shower.
  • Clear vs opaque: Clear liners let light into the shower; white fabric adds privacy.
  • Hooks: Most liners do not list hooks as included, so you may need to reuse yours or buy a set.
  • Air it out: Spread the liner fully closed after showering so it dries evenly instead of staying bunched and damp.
  • Ventilate: Run the bathroom fan or crack a window to help the whole room dry, which also helps the liner stay cleaner.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my shower curtain liner blow in toward me?

Hot shower air rises and creates a pressure difference that pulls a light liner inward. A heavier liner, a weighted hem or bottom magnets, and tucking the hem inside the tub all help keep it in place.

Are magnets or weights better on a shower curtain liner?

Weights generally hold the hem down by gravity on any tub, while magnets only grip a steel or cast iron tub. AmazerBath says its 3 big weighted stones add more weight than small magnets for better anti-billowing.

Is a fabric or plastic shower curtain liner better?

PEVA and vinyl liners are fully waterproof and wipe clean, while polyester fabric liners feel softer, have no plastic smell and can go in the washing machine. Choose fabric if you prefer washing over wiping.

How often should you replace a shower curtain liner?

Replace it when you see stains or buildup that no longer wipe or wash off, or if the header or grommets start to tear. Wiping a plastic liner or washing a fabric one regularly helps it last longer.

What size shower curtain liner do I need?

72 x 72 inches is the standard size and fits most standard tubs and showers. Measure your rod width and height first if you have an extra-wide or extra-tall shower.
