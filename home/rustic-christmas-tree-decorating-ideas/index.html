How to decorate a rustic Christmas tree, step by step

  1. Lights first. Use warm white lights and wrap them deep into the branches, not just around the outside. Warm light makes wood, knit and pinecones glow.
  2. Set the base. Lay the tree skirt (or a tree collar) before you add ornaments so you do not knock anything off later.
  3. Drape the garland. Start at the top and swag the wood bead garland loosely in shallow loops, working down and around the tree. Looser loops look more relaxed and rustic.
  4. Add ornaments in groups. Hang pinecones in small clusters of two or three rather than spacing them evenly. Tuck some deeper into the branches for depth.
  5. Finish with one accent. Add a few red buffalo check, burlap or jute ribbon bows, or a simple star topper. One accent color is enough.

Rustic tree styling tips

  • Limit the palette. Green, cream, natural wood and brown, plus one accent such as deep red, keep the tree calm and cohesive.
  • Mix textures, not colors. Knit, wood, pinecone scales, jute and plaid give a rustic tree its depth.
  • Repeat materials around the room. Use leftover wood bead garland on the mantel and a few pinecones in a bowl on the coffee table. Our Christmas mantel decor ideas has more ways to tie the room together.
  • Wrap gifts to match. Kraft paper, jute twine and a single pinecone on top look beautiful sitting on a cream knit skirt.
  • Consider a collar for a crisp edge. If you love a structured look, a woven tree collar does the same job as a skirt. See the seagrass collar in our farmhouse Christmas decor ideas.

Buying tips

  • Measure your tree's spread. A 48-inch skirt covers many 6 to 7.5 ft trees, but very full or 9 ft trees may need a larger size.
  • Plan garland length. Count on more than one 16-foot strand if you want full, looping swags on a tall tree.
  • Order early. Popular holiday decor can sell out or ship slowly in December.
  • Store with care. Fold the skirt flat, coil the garland around a piece of cardboard and keep pinecones in a box so they keep their shape for next year.

Frequently asked questions

How do you decorate a Christmas tree in a rustic style?

Start with natural materials and a short color palette. Use a knit, burlap or woven base instead of a shiny tree skirt, drape a wood bead garland instead of tinsel, and fill the branches with pinecones, wood and fabric ornaments. Warm white lights and a few red plaid or jute bows finish the look.

What colors are used for a rustic Christmas tree?

Rustic trees usually mix evergreen with cream, natural wood, warm brown and one accent color, most often a deep red or a red and black buffalo check. Keep metallics to a minimum and choose warm white lights rather than cool white or multicolor.

How much wood bead garland do I need for a Christmas tree?

A common rule of thumb is about 9 to 10 feet of garland per foot of tree height for full, looping swags, and less if you prefer a few loose drapes. The WILLBOND garland is 16 feet long, so plan on two or more strands for a full 7.5 ft tree.

Can you put real pinecones on a Christmas tree?

Yes. Dry pinecones are light, so they hang easily on most branches. The JOHOUSE set includes 18 natural pinecones in mixed sizes from about 1.5 to 2.5 inches, each with a ready-to-hang loop, so you do not need hooks or glue.

Is a knit tree skirt better than a tree collar?

It depends on the look you want. A tree collar is a stiff ring that hides the stand with a tidy, structured shape, while a knit tree skirt lies flat on the floor, adds soft texture and gives gifts a cozy place to sit. A knit skirt also folds flat for storage.
