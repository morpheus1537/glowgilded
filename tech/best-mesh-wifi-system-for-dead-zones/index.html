How to fix WiFi dead zones

  1. Test first: Walk your home with a phone speed test and note where the signal drops.
  2. Move the main unit: Put it in the open, off the floor and away from metal cabinets, microwaves and fish tanks.
  3. Place satellites halfway: Put the next unit roughly halfway between the main unit and the problem room, not inside the dead zone itself.
  4. Use Ethernet when you can: Both Deco systems support wired backhaul, which can help if your home already has Ethernet runs.
  5. Turn on the guest network: Give visitors their own network so you do not have to share your main password.
  6. Update regularly: Both brands push firmware updates through their apps.

Buying tips

Frequently asked questions

Is mesh WiFi better than a WiFi extender for dead zones?

For most homes, yes. A mesh system uses several units that work as one network, so devices move between them automatically. Extenders are cheaper but often create a separate network and can cut speeds. Mesh is the better fix if dead zones are spread across a home.

How many mesh WiFi units do I need?

Go by the coverage the manufacturer lists and the layout of your home. The eero 6 lists up to 1,500 sq. ft. for one unit, the Deco X55 up to 2,500 sq. ft. for one, the Deco X20 two-pack up to 4,000 sq. ft. and the eero 6+ four-pack up to 6,000 sq. ft. Thick walls and multiple floors may need an extra unit.

Do I need to replace my router to use mesh WiFi?

Usually the mesh system becomes your router, connected to your internet provider's modem. The TP-Link listing notes that a modem is required for most internet providers. If your provider gives you a combo modem-router, you may need to adjust its settings.

Will mesh WiFi make my internet faster?

Mesh WiFi will not raise the speed of your internet plan, but it can deliver more of that speed to rooms that had weak signal. Match the system to your plan; eero 6 supports plans up to 900 Mbps, while eero 6+ and the Deco X20 support plans up to about a gigabit.

Where should I put mesh WiFi units?

Put the main unit near your modem, then place the others roughly halfway between the main unit and the dead zone, out in the open rather than inside cabinets. Both the Deco and eero apps guide you through placement during setup.
