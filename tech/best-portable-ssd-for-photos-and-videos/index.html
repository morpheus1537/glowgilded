How to choose a portable SSD for photos and videos

  1. Speed: All four picks list USB 3.2 Gen 2 speeds of up to about 1,050MB/s read. You need a matching USB port to get full speed.
  2. Capacity: 1TB suits most phone libraries; 2TB or more suits frequent 4K video and RAW photos.
  3. Durability: Look for drop protection and an IP rating if you shoot outdoors or travel.
  4. Size and weight: Smaller drives are easier to carry and to use with a phone.
  5. Security: Password protection or encryption helps if the drive holds private files.
  6. Compatibility: Check that the drive works with your devices, such as Mac, PC, Android, iPhone, cameras and game consoles.

Photo backup tips

  • Use the 3-2-1 idea: Keep three copies of important photos, on two kinds of storage, with one stored away from home or in the cloud.
  • Use the right port: Plug into a USB 3.2 Gen 2 or faster port with the included cable for full speed.
  • Name folders by date: A simple year-month folder system makes photos easy to find later.
  • Eject before unplugging: Always eject the drive safely to avoid file errors.
  • Keep power handy: On a trip, a portable charger keeps your phone alive while you copy files.
  • Show them off: Load favorite shots onto a digital picture frame for grandparents.

Portable SSD vs. cloud storage

Cloud storage is convenient and protects photos if a drive is lost, but large video libraries can take a long time to upload and usually need an ongoing subscription. A portable SSD is a one-time purchase, works without internet and copies files much faster. Many people use both: an SSD for fast local backups and the cloud as an off-site copy.

Frequently asked questions

Is a portable SSD better than an external hard drive for photos?

For most people, yes. Portable SSDs have no moving parts, are much faster and are smaller than spinning hard drives. Hard drives still cost less per terabyte for very large archives.

How much storage do I need for photos and videos?

1TB is a good start for a phone or hobby camera library. If you shoot 4K video or RAW photos often, consider 2TB or more. The Samsung T7 is offered in capacities up to 4TB, and the SanDisk Extreme here is the 2TB version.

Can I record iPhone video directly to a portable SSD?

Some drives support it. Samsung says the T7 supports recording iPhone 15 Pro ProRes 4K at 60fps, and Lexar says the ES3 works with the iPhone 17 series but not iPhone 14 and older.

Are portable SSDs waterproof?

Some are water resistant, not waterproof. The Samsung T7 Shield and SanDisk Extreme both list an IP65 rating for water and dust resistance; the standard Samsung T7 does not list one.

Should I still keep a second backup?

Yes. A portable SSD is a great working drive or backup, but keeping another copy, such as a second drive or cloud storage, protects you if one drive is lost or fails.
