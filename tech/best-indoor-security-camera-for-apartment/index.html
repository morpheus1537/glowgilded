What to look for in an apartment security camera

  1. Field of view: A pan-tilt camera can cover a whole studio; a fixed camera is fine for a doorway or hallway.
  2. Resolution: 2K or 2.5K makes it easier to read details; 1080p is enough for checking on pets.
  3. Storage and fees: Decide whether you want free alerts, local microSD storage or a cloud plan.
  4. Privacy controls: A lens cover or scheduled privacy mode matters if you are home a lot.
  5. Voice assistant: Pick a camera that works with the Alexa or Google screen you already own.

Apartment camera tips

  • Respect privacy: Point cameras at your own space only, and tell roommates and guests where they are.
  • Mind the window glare: Night vision can reflect off glass, so avoid aiming at a window at night.
  • Use strong passwords: Turn on two-factor login in the camera app.
  • Plan power: A short extension or smart plug can help; see our best smart plug for Christmas lights for plug ideas.
  • Check your Wi-Fi: Cameras use 2.4 GHz or dual-band Wi-Fi; weak signal causes laggy live views.

Frequently asked questions

Can renters install indoor security cameras?

Usually, yes. Plug-in indoor cameras sit on a shelf or table and need no drilling or wiring, which is why they are popular with renters. Check your lease and local laws, and never point cameras at areas where neighbors or roommates expect privacy.

Do indoor security cameras need a subscription?

It depends on the brand. Tapo lists motion, person and baby crying alerts with no additional fee, while Blink and WYZE offer optional paid plans for cloud recording and smart alerts. Check what each plan includes before you buy.

Which indoor camera has a privacy cover?

The Ring Indoor Cam 2K has an easy-to-slide lens cover. Ring notes that the microphone stays on when the lens is covered, but you can disable it in the Ring app.

Can I record to a memory card instead of the cloud?

The WYZE Cam v4 lists continuous recording to a microSD card up to 512 GB, sold separately. That is useful if you want to avoid monthly cloud fees.

Is a pan-tilt camera worth it in a small apartment?

For open-plan studios and living rooms, yes. A pan-tilt camera like the Tapo C210 can sweep 360° horizontally and 114° vertically, so one camera can cover more of the room than a fixed lens.
