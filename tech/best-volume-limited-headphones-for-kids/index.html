How to choose volume-limited headphones for kids

  1. Volume limit: Look for a stated limit, usually 85 dB. Some models add a 74 dB toddler mode or a 94 dB travel mode.
  2. Wired or wireless: Wired headphones work with school Chromebooks and never need charging. Bluetooth is cord-free but needs recharging.
  3. Battery life: For wireless models, a long battery and a backup 3.5mm cable prevent meltdowns mid-flight.
  4. Fit and comfort: An adjustable headband and soft ear cushions matter for small heads and long trips.
  5. Durability: Kids bend and drop headphones. Look for flexible, sturdy materials.
  6. Microphone: Useful for online learning and calls with grandparents.

Tips for safe headphone use

  • Default to the lowest mode: Use 85 dB or 74 dB for everyday listening and save 94 dB for loud places like planes.
  • Set device limits too: iPads, Android tablets and Chromebooks all have volume and screen-time controls.
  • Take breaks: Short breaks every hour give ears a rest, especially on long trips.
  • Check the fit: Ear cups that seal well block outside noise, so kids are less tempted to turn the volume up.
  • Charge before trips: Charge wireless headphones the night before and pack the backup cable.
  • Label them: A name sticker helps at school, and JBL even includes a sticker set.

Wired vs Bluetooth for school

Many schools require wired headphones because they work instantly with Chromebooks and do not need to be paired or charged. The awatrue AT2 and JBL Junior 320 suit that use. At home and on trips, Bluetooth models like EarFun, iClever and JoySpark free kids from cords, and their 3.5mm backup means they can still be used wired. Frequently asked questions

What volume limit is safe for kids' headphones?

Most kids' headphones cap output at 85 dB, a level widely used for children's listening. Several picks here also offer a lower 74 dB mode for toddlers or a 94 dB mode for noisy places like planes, which should be used only when needed.

Are wired or Bluetooth headphones better for kids?

Wired headphones never need charging and plug straight into school Chromebooks and tablets. Bluetooth headphones remove the cord, and the wireless picks here include a 3.5mm cable as a backup when the battery runs out.

At what age can kids use headphones?

It depends on the headphones' fit. iClever says its BTH22 fits children aged 2 and up, while most others are aimed at school-age kids. Keep listening sessions short for young children and use the lowest volume mode.

Do kids' headphones need a microphone?

A built-in mic is useful for online classes, language apps and video calls with family. Every pick in this guide includes a microphone.

Can volume-limited headphones still get too loud?

The limit depends on the headphones and the device. Check that the volume-limit mode is set correctly, keep the device volume moderate, and set screen-time and volume limits on the tablet or phone too.
