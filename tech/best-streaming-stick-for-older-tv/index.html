Before you buy: check your older TV

  1. HDMI port: Every device here needs one. Most flat screens made in the last 15 years have at least one.
  2. Resolution: A 720p or 1080p TV cannot show 4K. An HD stick is enough, though a 4K stick still works.
  3. Space behind the TV: Sticks plug directly into the port. If ports sit close together or face the wall, a short HDMI extender can help.
  4. Power: Some sticks can run from the TV's USB port; others need a wall outlet. Check the listing.
  5. Wi-Fi strength: Older TVs often live in bedrooms or basements far from the router.

Setup tips for older TVs

  • Switch the input: Use the TV remote's Input or Source button to select the HDMI port you used.
  • Use a wall outlet if the stick keeps restarting: Some older TV USB ports do not supply enough power.
  • Turn on HDMI-CEC: Look for it in your TV's settings (brands call it Anynet+, Bravia Sync, SimpLink and so on) so the streaming remote can control power and volume.
  • Set the right resolution: Most sticks auto-detect, but check display settings if the picture looks stretched.

Frequently asked questions

Will a streaming stick work on an old TV?

Yes, as long as the TV has an HDMI port. Plug the stick into the HDMI port, connect it to Wi-Fi and sign in to your apps. TVs with only red, white and yellow composite inputs need a separate HDMI-to-AV converter, and picture quality will be limited.

Do I need a 4K streaming stick for a 1080p TV?

No. A 1080p TV can only show HD, so an HD stick like the Roku Streaming Stick HD is enough. A 4K stick still works on an HD TV and is worth it if you plan to upgrade to a 4K TV soon or want faster Wi-Fi.

Do streaming sticks have monthly fees?

The sticks themselves have no required subscription. You pay for services such as Netflix or Disney+ separately, and both Roku and Fire TV offer free, ad-supported movies and live TV channels.

Can the streaming stick remote turn my old TV on and off?

Often. The Roku remotes are listed as controlling TV power and volume, but this depends on your TV supporting the feature, so older sets may still need their own remote for power.

Which is easier for older relatives, Roku or Fire TV?

Roku is often recommended for its simple home screen and uncluttered remote, which Roku highlights in its listing. Fire TV is a strong choice for Alexa and Prime Video households. Both support voice search.
