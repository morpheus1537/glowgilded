How to choose a surge protector with USB

  1. Joule rating: This is how much surge energy it can absorb. More is better for computers and TVs.
  2. Outlet count and spacing: Count your plugs, then add one or two. Wide spacing fits bulky adapters.
  3. USB ports: Look for USB-C if you charge newer phones, and check the listed output per port.
  4. Cord or wall mount: A corded strip reaches across a room; a wall tap saves space.
  5. Safety certification: Look for UL or ETL listing and an indicator light that shows protection status.
  6. Warranty: Some brands add a connected equipment warranty.

Surge protector safety tips

  • Plug directly into the wall: Do not plug one strip into another.
  • Watch the indicator light: If protection shows as off, replace the unit.
  • Do not overload it: Space heaters and other high-draw appliances should go straight into a wall outlet.
  • Keep it dry and ventilated: Do not cover strips with rugs or bedding.
  • Replace old units: Protection wears down with each surge, so replace aging strips.
  • Smart control: For holiday lights, a smart plug for Christmas lights adds scheduling.

Frequently asked questions

What joule rating should a surge protector have?

Higher joules mean more surge energy absorbed before the protector wears out. Around 1,000 joules or more is a common choice for computers and TVs; the picks here range from 490 to 1,800 joules.

Are USB ports on a surge protector good for charging phones?

Yes, for everyday charging. Check the listed output. For example, the Belkin pick has a USB-C Power Delivery port, while others list USB-A ports at up to 2.4A.

Can I plug a surge protector into another power strip?

No. Plugging surge protectors or power strips into each other (daisy-chaining) is a common safety warning. Plug each one directly into a wall outlet.

Do surge protectors wear out?

Yes. Each surge uses up some protection. Many models, like the Belkin and Addtam picks here, have an indicator light that shows whether protection is still active.

Wall-mount or corded surge protector?

A wall-mount (outlet extender) is compact and great behind nightstands or on counters. A corded strip, like the Amazon Basics 6-foot model, reaches a desk or TV stand away from the outlet.
