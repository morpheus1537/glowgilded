How to choose a smart plug for Christmas lights

  • Indoor or outdoor: Any plug that sits outside should have a weather rating such as IP44 or IP64. Indoor plugs are fine for trees and window displays.
  • Individual control: Multi-outlet outdoor plugs either control each outlet separately (Kasa EP40, meross) or all together (BN-LINK). Separate control lets you set different schedules.
  • Sunset scheduling: Dusk-based scheduling means you never have to adjust timers as the days get shorter.
  • Your ecosystem: Check that the plug works with your assistant: Alexa, Google Assistant or Apple HomeKit.
  • Wi-Fi reach: Outdoor plugs need a solid 2.4GHz signal at the outlet. Test your phone's Wi-Fi at the spot before you buy.
  • Load: Add up the wattage of everything you'll plug in and stay within the plug's rating. LED lights use far less power than incandescent strings.

Smart plug safety tips

  • Keep outdoor connections off the ground and out of puddles, and use covers on unused outlets.
  • Use only outdoor-rated extension cords outside.
  • Don't daisy-chain multiple power strips into one smart plug.
  • Unplug and inspect lights with frayed wires or cracked sockets.

Frequently asked questions

Can you use a smart plug for Christmas lights?

Yes. Plug your lights into a smart plug and you can switch them on and off from your phone, by voice with Alexa or Google Assistant, or automatically on a schedule. For lights outside, use a smart plug rated for outdoor use.

Do I need an outdoor smart plug for outdoor Christmas lights?

If the plug will sit outside, yes. Choose one with a weather-resistance rating, such as the Kasa EP40 (IP64) or BN-LINK outdoor plug (IP44), and keep connections off the ground and out of standing water.

Can a smart plug turn Christmas lights on at sunset?

Many can. The Kasa EP40 supports sunset and sunrise offset scheduling, and the meross outdoor plug supports sunrise and sunset settings, so lights follow the real time of dusk as the season changes.

Do smart plugs work on 5GHz Wi-Fi?

Most do not. The Kasa, BN-LINK and meross plugs in this guide require a 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network. Most routers broadcast 2.4GHz alongside 5GHz, so you can usually connect without changing anything.

Which smart plug works with Apple HomeKit?

The meross outdoor smart plug in this guide is Apple HomeKit compatible. Remote control through HomeKit needs a home hub such as a HomePod or Apple TV.
