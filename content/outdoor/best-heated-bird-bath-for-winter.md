When the temperature drops below freezing, food is not the only thing birds struggle to find. Puddles, birdbaths and ponds ice over, and open water becomes scarce. A heated bird bath or bird bath de-icer keeps water available all winter, and it often draws in species you never see at your feeders.

## Heated bird bath or de-icer?

- **Heated bird bath:** A complete basin with a built-in heater. Easiest if you do not already own a bath, and you can mount it on a deck railing or stand.
- **De-icer:** A small heater that sits in the water of a bath you already own. Easiest if you have a favorite bath or a concrete fountain.

## How to choose

1. **Wattage:** Lower wattage uses less electricity; higher wattage suits larger or colder setups.
2. **Thermostat:** All four here are thermostatically controlled to run only when needed.
3. **Mounting:** Deck mount, clamp or ground stand options make placement easier.
4. **Bath material:** De-icers should be safe for your bath's material.
5. **Depth and perching:** Shallow basins and grippable rims are friendlier to small birds.
6. **Power:** Plan for a GFCI-protected outdoor outlet and an outdoor-rated cord.

## Winter bird bath tips

- **Keep it clean:** Scrub the basin regularly and refill with fresh water.
- **Keep it shallow:** One to two inches of water is plenty for most songbirds.
- **Watch the water level:** De-icers must stay covered, and water evaporates faster than you think.
- **Use safe power:** Plug into a GFCI-protected outlet and use an outdoor-rated cord.
- **Skip antifreeze:** Never add chemicals to keep water open.
- **Place near cover:** Shrubs and trees nearby help birds escape predators.
- **Track the cold:** A [wireless indoor-outdoor thermometer](/outdoor/best-wireless-indoor-outdoor-thermometer/) shows when temperatures dip below freezing.
