The extension cord is the least exciting part of a Christmas light display, and the part most likely to cause trouble. An indoor cord can crack in the cold, a cord with one outlet at the end means a tangle of splitters, and a bright orange cord ruins a beautiful yard photo. ## How to choose an outdoor extension cord for Christmas lights

1. **Outdoor rating:** Look for an outdoor or SJTW-rated jacket that resists moisture, abrasion and sunlight.
2. **Gauge and rating:** 16-gauge cords rated 13 amps and 1625 watts handle most LED displays.
3. **Outlets:** Multiple or evenly spaced outlets mean fewer cords and splitters.
4. **Color:** Green blends into grass and shrubs; black hides along dark trim and mulch.
5. **Cold flexibility:** A cord that stays soft in winter is easier to route.
6. **Safety listing:** Choose ETL-listed or similarly certified cords.

## Outdoor Christmas light safety tips

- **Use a GFCI outlet:** Plug outdoor displays into a GFCI-protected outdoor outlet.
- **Do the math:** Add up the wattage of everything on a cord and stay under its rating.
- **Keep connections up:** Lift plugs off the ground, away from snow and puddles.
- **Do not daisy-chain:** Use one longer cord rather than many short ones linked together.
- **Inspect each year:** Replace cords with cracked jackets or bent prongs.
- **Prefer smart control?** Our [best smart plug for Christmas lights](/tech/best-smart-plug-for-christmas-lights/) guide covers app and voice options.

## Cord color: green, black or orange?

Green is best for lawns, shrubs and garland. Black blends into dark trim, railings and mulch. Bright orange is the most visible, which is safer around work areas but stands out in a holiday display. For most Christmas lights, choose green or black.
