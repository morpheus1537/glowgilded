Before you head out, it tells you whether you need a heavy coat, whether the walkway might be icy, and whether it is cold enough to protect plants or pipes. ## What to look for in a wireless thermometer

1. **Range:** How far the outdoor sensor can be from the display. Longer range helps with detached garages or sheds.
2. **Update interval:** Most sensors send new readings every 30 seconds or so.
3. **High and low records:** Useful for checking how cold it got overnight.
4. **Humidity:** A hygrometer helps you manage dry winter air indoors.
5. **Display and power:** A large, backlit or USB-powered display is easier to read in the dark.
6. **Extra sensors:** Multi-sensor kits can watch the nursery, basement and garage at once.

## Setup tips for accurate readings

- **Shade the sensor:** Direct sun can make an outdoor sensor read many degrees too high.
- **Keep it dry:** Mount it under an eave or in a small shelter, even if it is weather-resistant.
- **Avoid heat sources:** Stay away from dryer vents, grills, AC units and dark walls.
- **Test the range first:** Set up the display and sensor in their final spots before mounting anything.
- **Swap batteries before deep winter:** Cold weather drains batteries faster.
- **Plan for snow days:** Pair your thermometer with the right [snow removal tools](/outdoor/snow-removal-tools/).
