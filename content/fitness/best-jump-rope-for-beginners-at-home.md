## How to choose a jump rope as a beginner

1. **Rope weight and thickness:** Thicker PVC or lightly weighted ropes are easier to feel and time; thin steel cables are fast but less forgiving.
2. **Adjustable length:** You will need to size the rope to your height, so adjustable is a must.
3. **Handles:** Foam or silicone grips stay comfortable when your hands sweat.
4. **Bearings:** Ball bearings help the rope spin smoothly without tangling.
5. **Surface:** Some ropes handle rough outdoor surfaces; others are best on smooth floors or mats.
6. **Tracking:** A built-in counter can keep you motivated.

## Jump rope tips for beginners

- **Size it first:** Stand on the middle of the rope; the handles should reach about your armpits. Shorten gradually as you improve.
- **Use a mat:** A [thick yoga or exercise mat](/fitness/best-thick-yoga-mat-for-bad-knees/) protects floors and cushions landings, though a firm rubber mat is better than very soft foam.
- **Check your space:** Make sure you have clearance overhead and around you, away from lamps and furniture.
- **Jump low:** Small hops of an inch or two are all you need. Land softly on the balls of your feet.
- **Turn from the wrists:** Keep your elbows close to your sides and let your wrists do the work.
- **Wear supportive shoes:** Cushioned trainers are kinder to your feet and joints than bare feet.
- **Start with intervals:** Jump for 30 seconds, rest for 30, and repeat for five to ten minutes.

## A simple first-week plan

Day 1 and 2: practice the basic bounce for 30-second rounds with 30-second rests, five rounds. Day 3: rest or walk. Day 4 and 5: six to eight rounds, and try alternating feet like jogging in place. Day 6: try a counter rope countdown or a set number of jumps. Day 7: rest. Increase rounds slowly as your calves and shins adapt.
