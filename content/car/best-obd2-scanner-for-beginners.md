## How to choose an OBD2 scanner as a beginner

1. **Bluetooth adapter or handheld:** Adapters use your phone and an app; handhelds have their own screen.
2. **Code explanations:** Plain-English definitions and repair suggestions help most beginners.
3. **Subscriptions:** Some apps charge extra; check what is included.
4. **Live data and readiness:** Useful for checking repairs and preparing for inspections.
5. **Advanced systems:** ABS, airbag and TPMS codes need an enhanced scanner or app.
6. **Vehicle compatibility:** Check model years, diesels, EVs and security gateway notes.

## How to use an OBD2 scanner

- **Find the port:** It is usually under the dashboard near the steering column.
- **Key on, engine off:** Plug in, turn the ignition on and follow the scanner or app.
- **Write down codes first:** Record codes and freeze frame data before clearing anything.
- **Research before replacing parts:** One code can have several causes.
- **Fix, then clear:** Clear codes only after the repair and recheck after a few drives.
- **Know when to stop:** A flashing check engine light signals a serious misfire; reduce driving and get it checked.
- **Keep a winter kit ready:** A [portable jump starter](/car/best-portable-jump-starter-for-cold-weather/) and [tire inflator](/car/best-tire-inflator-for-cold-weather/) pair well with a scanner.

## Common beginner codes

P0455 and P0457 often point to an evaporative emissions leak such as a loose gas cap. P0300-P0308 indicate misfires, and P0420 relates to catalytic converter efficiency. Your scanner or app will show definitions, but always confirm the cause before buying parts.
