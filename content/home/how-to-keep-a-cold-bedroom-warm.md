A cold bedroom is one of the hardest winter problems to fix, because it is the room where you notice every draft. You get into chilly sheets, feel cold air sliding across the floor from the hallway and wake up at 3 AM with a cold nose. Turning up the thermostat for the whole house just to warm one room is expensive and often does not help much if the heat is leaking out of the window and under the door.

The better approach is to work in layers: block the cold that is getting in, then warm the bed itself. ## How to warm a cold bedroom, step by step

1. **Find the drafts.** On a windy evening, hold your hand near the window frame, outlets on exterior walls and the bottom of the door. Wherever you feel moving air, that is where to start.
2. **Seal the door gap.** A door sweep costs little and stops cold hallway air from flowing across the floor.
3. **Cover the windows at night.** Close heavy curtains at dusk, and open them on sunny days to let natural heat in.
4. **Change the bedding.** Flannel sheets and a heavier comforter make a big difference without any electricity.
5. **Preheat the bed.** Turn on a heated mattress pad 15 to 30 minutes before bed, then lower the setting or let the timer switch it off.
6. **Add a rug.** Bare floors feel cold underfoot. A rug beside the bed helps you avoid that first icy step.

## Buying tips

- **Measure first:** Check your mattress depth before buying a heated pad or fitted sheet, and measure your window width and rod height before ordering curtains.
- **Choose dual control for couples:** If one person runs hot and the other runs cold, a dual-control pad avoids nightly arguments.
- **Look for auto shut-off:** Both heated pads here include timers. Use them, and follow the manufacturer's care and safety directions.
- **Start with the cheapest fix:** A draft stopper is inexpensive and may solve more of the problem than you expect.
- **Check the current listing:** Sizes and colors vary, and ratings can differ between variants.
