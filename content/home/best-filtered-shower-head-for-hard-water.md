## How to choose a filtered shower head for hard water

1. **Filter media:** Look for what the cartridge contains. KDF-55, calcium sulfite, activated carbon and vitamin C are common; each targets different things, mainly chlorine and odors.
2. **Head style:** Fixed heads are simplest. Rain heads feel luxurious. A handheld wand makes rinsing kids, pets and the tub easier.
3. **Spray modes:** More modes mean more choice, from mist to massage.
4. **Fit:** Nearly all US shower arms use standard 1/2-inch threads. All five picks here say they fit standard arms.
5. **Replacement filters:** Check that refills are easy to find and how long each lasts.
6. **Inline vs. all-in-one:** An inline filter like Aquabliss keeps your current head; a combo replaces it.

## Hard water shower tips

- **Flush new filters:** Run water through a new cartridge for several minutes before your first shower.
- **Use tape:** Wrap the shower arm threads with thread seal tape to prevent drips.
- **Mark the date:** Write the install date on your phone calendar so you remember to replace the cartridge.
- **Clean the nozzles:** Wipe the spray face regularly to help keep mineral buildup off the nozzles.
- **Finish with moisture:** Hard water and dry winter air are a tough combination; see our [winter hair care for dry hair](/beauty/winter-hair-care-for-dry-hair/) guide for the rest of the routine.

## Filtered shower head vs. whole-house water softener

A shower filter is an affordable, renter-friendly fix for one bathroom, and most focus on chlorine and odors. A whole-house water softener uses a different process to remove hardness minerals from all of your water, but it needs plumbing work and more upkeep. If scale on faucets and appliances is your main problem, a softener is the bigger solution; if you mainly want a nicer shower, a filtered head is the easy place to start.
