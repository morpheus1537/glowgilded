## How to choose an air purifier for your bedroom

1. **CADR (clean air delivery rate):** The higher the CADR, the faster it cleans. Look for an AHAM-verified number.
2. **Room size:** Check the square footage the maker lists for one or more air changes per hour.
3. **Sleep mode noise:** Look for a listed decibel figure for the lowest setting.
4. **Light control:** A display you can turn off matters in a dark bedroom.
5. **Filter type:** HEPA-grade filters capture fine particles; carbon layers help with odors.
6. **Footprint:** Small rooms need a purifier that fits by the bed or on a dresser.

## Bedroom air purifier buying tips

- **Size up, then turn down:** A purifier rated for a bigger room can run on a quieter low setting and still clean well.
- **Run it all the time:** Purifiers work best running continuously with doors and windows closed.
- **Give it space:** Keep a foot or two of clearance around the intake and outlet.
- **Budget for filters:** Check replacement filter availability before you buy, and change them when the indicator tells you.
- **Pair it with the right humidity:** Dry winter air can feel harsh. A humidifier helps, and our [cool mist vs warm mist guide](/home/cool-mist-vs-warm-mist-heated-apartments/) explains which type suits heated apartments.
- **Mind the temperature:** Purifiers move air but do not heat it; see [how to keep a cold bedroom warm](/home/how-to-keep-a-cold-bedroom-warm/) for winter comfort.

## Air purifiers and allergies

Air purifiers can reduce airborne dust, pollen and pet dander, but they do not remove dust that has settled on bedding, carpets and furniture. Wash sheets weekly in hot water, vacuum regularly and keep pets off the bed if allergies are a problem. If you have asthma or severe allergies, talk to your doctor about what will help most.
