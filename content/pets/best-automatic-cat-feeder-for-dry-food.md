An automatic cat feeder keeps meals on schedule when you work late, sleep in on weekends or head out for a holiday visit. It can also help cats who wake you at 5 AM demanding breakfast, because the feeder, not you, becomes the source of food. For dry kibble, a good feeder needs reliable portions, a hopper that keeps food fresh and a backup plan if the power goes out.

## How to choose an automatic cat feeder

1. **Capacity:** A larger hopper means fewer refills, but food stays freshest when you fill only what you need.
2. **Portion size:** Small portions (about 7 to 10 grams) give you fine control over each meal.
3. **Meals per day:** Most feeders allow up to 6 meals; WiFi models may allow more.
4. **Backup power:** Batteries keep meals coming during outages.
5. **Freshness:** Look for a sealed lid and desiccant to keep kibble crisp.
6. **Cleaning:** Removable, dishwasher-safe bowls and hoppers save time.
7. **Kibble size:** Check the maximum kibble size so food does not jam.

## Tips for using an automatic feeder

- **Test before you travel:** Run the feeder for a few days while you are home to confirm portions and timing.
- **Use fresh batteries:** Add backup batteries before any trip.
- **Fill only what you need:** Smaller fills keep kibble fresher.
- **Keep water flowing:** Pair the feeder with a fountain. See our [best stainless steel cat water fountains](/pets/best-stainless-steel-cat-water-fountain/).
- **Clean weekly:** Wash bowls and wipe the hopper to remove oily residue.
- **Arrange check-ins:** Feeders do not replace a sitter for litter, water and health checks.
- **Watch weight:** Portions on a feeder are easy to adjust; ask your vet how much your cat should eat.
