## How to choose a deshedding brush for your cat

1. **Coat length:** Deshedding blades work best on short coats. Long-haired cats usually need a rake or slicker that will not pull tangles.
2. **Cat size:** FURminator sizes its tools by weight, so choose large for cats over 10 pounds.
3. **Gentleness:** Look for a skin guard, rounded or rubber-tipped pins and a light touch.
4. **Cleaning:** A release button pushes fur off the brush in seconds.
5. **Your cat's temperament:** Nervous cats often accept a soft slicker before a stronger deshedding tool.

## How to deshed a cat without stress

- **Start small:** Brush for a minute or two at first, then end on a good note with a treat.
- **Go with the grain:** Brush in the direction the hair grows, using light pressure.
- **Avoid sensitive spots:** Skip the belly, legs and tail unless your cat is relaxed.
- **Do not force mats:** Brushing over a tight mat can pull skin. Ask a groomer or vet for help with severe mats.
- **Pick a calm time:** Many cats are most relaxed after a meal or nap.
- **Clean up after:** Brush on a towel or in an easy-to-vacuum room, then use a lint roller on yourself.
- **Give them a place to scratch:** A sturdy post like those in our [best cat scratching post for indoor cats](/pets/best-cat-scratching-post-for-indoor-cats/) guide helps keep claws healthy too.
