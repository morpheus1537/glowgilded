## How to choose a scratching post

1. **Height:** Look for a post tall enough for a full stretch. Around 32 to 34 inches suits most adult cats.
2. **Stability:** A wide, heavy base stops the post from tipping. A wobbly post can scare a cat away for good.
3. **Material:** Woven sisal and sisal rope are tough and satisfying. Corrugated cardboard suits cats who like to shred.
4. **Vertical or horizontal:** Some cats scratch upward, others prefer the floor. An L-shaped scratcher offers both.
5. **Toys:** A dangling ball or mouse can lure a curious cat to the post.
6. **Post thickness:** Large cats benefit from a thicker post they can wrap their paws around.

## Tips to get your cat to use a scratching post

- **Place it by the problem spot:** Put the new post right next to the furniture your cat already scratches.
- **Use catnip or treats:** Rub a little catnip on the post or reward your cat when it scratches there.
- **Play near it:** Drag a wand toy up the post so your cat's claws sink into the sisal.
- **Make the couch less appealing:** Cover favorite scratching spots with a throw while your cat learns.
- **Never force paws onto the post:** Let your cat discover it on its own terms.
- **Trim nails regularly:** Shorter nails do less damage, and our [winter cat essentials](/pets/winter-cat-essentials/) cover more ways to keep an indoor cat happy.
