Big cats like Maine Coons, ragdolls and chunky house cats need more from a cat tree than a standard tower can give. Small perches leave tails hanging, narrow condos are a squeeze, and a light base can wobble when a 15-pound cat jumps on. A sturdy, roomy tree gives large cats a safe place to climb, scratch, nap and watch the world, and it saves your sofa from their claws.

## How to choose a cat tree for large cats

1. **Base size and weight:** A wide, heavy base is the best defense against wobbling.
2. **Perch and condo size:** Measure your cat stretched out; large cats need perches around 20 inches wide.
3. **Anti-tip hardware:** A wall strap or anti-tip kit is a must for tall trees.
4. **Sisal posts:** More, thicker posts give more scratching surface.
5. **Height:** Tall trees are great for lookouts; make sure the ceiling and room allow it.
6. **Cleaning:** Removable or washable parts make hair and dander easier to manage.

## Large cat tree tips

- **Anchor it:** Always use the anti-tip kit, and place tall trees in a corner or against a wall.
- **Pick a window:** Cats love a view; a tree by a window gets the most use.
- **Check bolts:** Re-tighten screws every few months as cats climb.
- **Vacuum often:** Plush collects fur; a quick vacuum keeps it fresh. For the rest of the house, see [how to get pet hair off the couch](/pets/how-to-get-pet-hair-off-couch-before-guests/).
- **Protect floors from litter, too:** A [cat litter mat](/pets/best-cat-litter-mat-for-tracking/) stops tracking near the box.

## Cat tree vs. wall shelves

Wall-mounted shelves save floor space and look sleek, but they need drilling into studs and a careful layout for heavy cats. A freestanding cat tree combines climbing, scratching, napping and hiding in one piece, needs no drilling and can move with you. For most renters and large cats, a sturdy tree is the easier choice.
