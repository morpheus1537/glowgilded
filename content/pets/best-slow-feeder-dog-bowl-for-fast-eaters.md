Some dogs inhale dinner in seconds, then look up as if they have not eaten in days. Gulping can lead to vomiting right after meals and leaves your dog bored for the rest of the evening. A slow feeder bowl uses ridges, a maze or raised bumps to make your dog work for every bite, turning a 20-second meal into a few minutes of focused, satisfying effort. With holiday guests and busy days ahead, that extra calm at mealtime helps.

## How to choose a slow feeder

1. **Capacity:** Choose a bowl that fits a full meal. Small and medium dogs often eat from 2-cup bowls; large breeds need more.
2. **Difficulty:** Deep, tight mazes slow dogs the most. Start easier for puppies, seniors or dogs with short snouts.
3. **Material:** Plastic is light and inexpensive; ceramic and stainless steel are heavier and chew resistant.
4. **Non-slip base:** A base that grips stops the bowl from sliding across the floor.
5. **Cleaning:** Mazes trap food, so dishwasher-safe bowls save time.

## Tips for fast eaters

- **Start easy:** Let your dog learn the bowl with a simple pattern, then increase the challenge.
- **Measure meals:** A slow feeder helps with pace, not portions. Use a measuring cup.
- **Split meals:** Two or three smaller meals a day can also help gulpers.
- **Supervise at first:** Watch for chewing or frustration in the first few meals.
- **Add enrichment:** Lick mats and puzzle toys extend the calm. See our [holiday dog enrichment ideas](/pets/holiday-dog-enrichment-guests/).
- **Talk to your vet:** Frequent vomiting or sudden changes in appetite deserve a checkup.
