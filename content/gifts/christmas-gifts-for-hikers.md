## How to choose a gift for a hiker

1. **Think light and packable:** Hikers count ounces, so compact gifts win.
2. **Match the season:** Winter hikers will love warmth, like socks and hand warmers. Summer hikers may prefer optics or poles.
3. **Avoid duplicates:** If they already own poles or binoculars, choose socks, a tool or a stocking stuffer.
4. **Consider experience:** Beginners benefit from basics like poles, while seasoned hikers appreciate upgrades and spares.
5. **Check travel rules:** If they fly to trailheads, TSA-friendly items like the UST carabiner and collapsible poles are a plus.

## Gift tips for hikers

- **Pair gifts:** Socks plus hand warmers make an easy winter bundle. Poles plus a carabiner tool suit a beginner.
- **Include a gift receipt:** Hikers are picky about fit and weight, so make exchanges easy.
- **Add a trail note:** Tuck in a printed map of a favorite local trail or a promise of a hike together.
- **Think safety:** Gifts like a whistle, warmers and a [rechargeable hand warmer](/outdoor/best-rechargeable-hand-warmers/) backup are thoughtful for winter hikers.
- **Wrap smart:** Use a stuff sack or buff as gift wrap that they can reuse on the trail.

## Gifts by budget and experience

For a new hiker, start with trekking poles, the gift with the biggest day-to-day impact, and add a pair of merino socks. For a seasoned hiker who already has the basics, choose upgrades and spares, like compact binoculars for scanning views or a fresh 3-pack of socks. For a stocking or a small coworker gift, the UST carabiner tool and the paracord bracelets are compact and inexpensive enough to add to a larger present.
