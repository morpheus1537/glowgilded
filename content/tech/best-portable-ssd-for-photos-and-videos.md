A portable SSD is the easiest fix: it fits in a pocket, plugs in over USB-C and copies a full day of photos and video in a fraction of the time an old hard drive takes. ## How to choose a portable SSD for photos and videos

1. **Speed:** All four picks list USB 3.2 Gen 2 speeds of up to about 1,050MB/s read. You need a matching USB port to get full speed.
2. **Capacity:** 1TB suits most phone libraries; 2TB or more suits frequent 4K video and RAW photos.
3. **Durability:** Look for drop protection and an IP rating if you shoot outdoors or travel.
4. **Size and weight:** Smaller drives are easier to carry and to use with a phone.
5. **Security:** Password protection or encryption helps if the drive holds private files.
6. **Compatibility:** Check that the drive works with your devices, such as Mac, PC, Android, iPhone, cameras and game consoles.

## Photo backup tips

- **Use the 3-2-1 idea:** Keep three copies of important photos, on two kinds of storage, with one stored away from home or in the cloud.
- **Use the right port:** Plug into a USB 3.2 Gen 2 or faster port with the included cable for full speed.
- **Name folders by date:** A simple year-month folder system makes photos easy to find later.
- **Eject before unplugging:** Always eject the drive safely to avoid file errors.
- **Keep power handy:** On a trip, a [portable charger](/tech/best-portable-charger-for-iphone/) keeps your phone alive while you copy files.
- **Show them off:** Load favorite shots onto a [digital picture frame for grandparents](/tech/digital-picture-frame-for-grandparents/).

## Portable SSD vs. cloud storage

Cloud storage is convenient and protects photos if a drive is lost, but large video libraries can take a long time to upload and usually need an ongoing subscription. A portable SSD is a one-time purchase, works without internet and copies files much faster. Many people use both: an SSD for fast local backups and the cloud as an off-site copy.
