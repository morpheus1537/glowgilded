Plug in a charger and you have one port left for a monitor, a thumb drive, an SD card and a wired mouse. ## How to choose a USB-C hub for a MacBook

1. **Ports you actually need:** Count your devices: HDMI, USB-A, USB-C, SD/microSD and Ethernet.
2. **Display output:** 4K at 60Hz is smoother than 4K at 30Hz for everyday work.
3. **Pass-through charging:** Look for a USB-C PD input and check the wattage passed to the laptop.
4. **Data speed:** 5Gbps is fine for most drives; 10Gbps helps with large video files.
5. **Fit:** Some hubs plug into both MacBook ports at once and can block MagSafe on certain models.
6. **Build:** Aluminum shells help with heat and look at home next to a MacBook.

## USB-C hub buying tips

- **Check your MacBook's display limits:** Base M1, M2 and M3 MacBook Air models support one external display; check Apple's specs before planning a dual-monitor setup.
- **Use a strong charger:** A hub takes some power for itself, so pair it with a charger that has headroom. Our [charging station guide](/tech/best-charging-station-for-multiple-devices/) covers multi-device chargers.
- **Mind the cable length:** Short built-in cables keep hubs compact but can pull on the port; avoid hanging heavy cables from them.
- **Plan for heat:** Hubs get warm with charging and video. Give them airflow and avoid stacking them under papers.
- **Raise your screen:** If you add an external monitor, a [monitor arm for a small desk](/office/best-monitor-arm-for-small-desk/) saves space.

## Hub vs. dock

A hub is small, bus-powered and travels with your laptop. A full dock is larger, has its own power supply and usually adds more ports and multiple displays. For a MacBook Air that moves between home, office and coffee shop, a compact hub is usually the better fit.
