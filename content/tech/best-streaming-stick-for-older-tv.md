It is also one of the easiest holiday upgrades for parents or grandparents whose TV still works perfectly but has no apps, or whose built-in smart features have stopped getting updates.

## Before you buy: check your older TV

1. **HDMI port:** Every device here needs one. Most flat screens made in the last 15 years have at least one.
2. **Resolution:** A 720p or 1080p TV cannot show 4K. An HD stick is enough, though a 4K stick still works.
3. **Space behind the TV:** Sticks plug directly into the port. If ports sit close together or face the wall, a short HDMI extender can help.
4. **Power:** Some sticks can run from the TV's USB port; others need a wall outlet. Check the listing.
5. **Wi-Fi strength:** Older TVs often live in bedrooms or basements far from the router.

## Setup tips for older TVs

- **Switch the input:** Use the TV remote's Input or Source button to select the HDMI port you used.
- **Use a wall outlet if the stick keeps restarting:** Some older TV USB ports do not supply enough power.
- **Turn on HDMI-CEC:** Look for it in your TV's settings (brands call it Anynet+, Bravia Sync, SimpLink and so on) so the streaming remote can control power and volume.
- **Set the right resolution:** Most sticks auto-detect, but check display settings if the picture looks stretched.
