Sunscreen is the step many people skip in winter, and it is the one dermatologists most want you to keep. Mineral sunscreens with zinc oxide are a favorite for sensitive skin, but untinted versions can leave a chalky white cast. A tinted mineral sunscreen solves that: it protects, evens out skin tone and can stand in for a light foundation on busy mornings.

## How to choose a tinted mineral sunscreen for your face

1. **SPF and broad spectrum:** Choose broad spectrum SPF 30 or higher for daily wear.
2. **Mineral filters:** Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the mineral (physical) filters.
3. **Shade range:** Check whether it comes in multiple shades or blends into all tones.
4. **Finish:** Dewy and moisturizing suits dry skin; matte suits oily skin.
5. **Skin type:** Look for fragrance-free and non-comedogenic if you are sensitive or acne-prone.
6. **Makeup:** Some double as primers under foundation.

## How to use tinted sunscreen for the best results

- **Use enough:** Apply a generous, even layer; a thin layer gives less protection than the label SPF.
- **Apply last in skincare:** Put it on after serum and moisturizer, before makeup.
- **Reapply:** Reapply every 2 hours outdoors and after sweating or swimming.
- **Do not forget your neck and ears:** They get sun too.
- **Layer smart:** A [vitamin C serum](/beauty/best-vitamin-c-serum-for-dark-spots/) under sunscreen is a classic morning combination.
- **Protect lips:** Winter sun and wind dry lips; see our [winter lip care essentials](/beauty/winter-lip-care-essentials/).

## Mineral vs. chemical sunscreen

Mineral sunscreens use zinc oxide and/or titanium dioxide, which many people with sensitive skin prefer. Chemical sunscreens use filters that absorb UV and often feel lighter with no white cast. Both protect when used correctly. Tinted mineral formulas bridge the gap by hiding the white cast.
