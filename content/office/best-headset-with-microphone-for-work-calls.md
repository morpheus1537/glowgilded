On a work call, people care more about how you sound than how you look. A laptop's built-in microphone picks up keyboard clicks, echo and the dishwasher, and laptop speakers can create feedback. A headset with a boom microphone puts the mic close to your mouth and the audio in your ears, so colleagues hear you clearly and you hear them without turning everything up.

## How to choose a headset for work calls

1. **Microphone:** A boom mic with noise cancelling picks up your voice and reduces background sound.
2. **Connection:** USB is plug-and-play and reliable. Bluetooth is wireless and can pair with a phone.
3. **Controls:** In-line or on-ear mute and volume controls save you from hunting for the on-screen button.
4. **Comfort:** Padded ear cushions and a light headband matter for all-day wear.
5. **Mono or stereo:** Stereo (both ears) blocks more room noise. Single-ear designs let you hear your surroundings.
6. **Compatibility:** Check your computer ports. These wired picks use USB-A, so newer laptops may need a USB-C adapter.

## Tips for better-sounding calls

- **Position the mic:** Keep the boom a couple of finger-widths from the corner of your mouth, not directly in front.
- **Select it in the app:** Choose the headset as both microphone and speaker in Zoom, Teams or Meet settings.
- **Test before meetings:** Use your app's audio test to check levels.
- **Reduce echo:** Rugs, curtains and bookshelves soften a bare room.
- **Mute when not talking:** Use the headset or in-line mute so others do not hear typing.
- **Light your face too:** Good audio and good lighting together make calls feel professional; see our [video call lighting setup](/office/video-call-lighting-setup/) guide.

## USB vs Bluetooth for working from home

Wired USB headsets are the most dependable choice for long days of back-to-back meetings. They draw power from the computer, do not drop connection and do not need pairing. Bluetooth headsets give you freedom to stand up, grab coffee or switch to a phone call, but they need charging and can be trickier to set up with desktop apps. Many remote workers keep a USB headset at the desk and use earbuds or a Bluetooth headset on the go.
