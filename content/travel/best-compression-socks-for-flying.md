## How to choose compression socks for flying

1. **Compression level:** Look for the mmHg rating. 15-20 mmHg is a common everyday and travel level, while 20-30 mmHg is firmer.
2. **Graduated design:** Graduated socks apply the most pressure at the ankle and less higher up, which is what you want for long periods of sitting.
3. **Size by calf, not just shoe:** Measure the widest part of your calf. A sock that is too tight can dig in, and one that is too loose does little.
4. **Length:** Knee-high socks are the standard for travel and cover the whole calf.
5. **Fabric:** Breathable nylon and spandex blends feel cooler on long flights and dry faster if you wash them in a hotel sink.
6. **Pack size:** Multi-pair packs let you wear a fresh pair on the way home.

## Tips for wearing compression socks on a flight

- **Put them on early:** Pull them on in the morning before your legs have a chance to swell.
- **Smooth out wrinkles:** Bunched fabric creates uneven pressure. Smooth the sock from ankle to knee.
- **Never fold the top band:** Rolling the cuff down concentrates pressure in one place.
- **Move when you can:** Walk the aisle, flex your ankles and do seated calf raises during long flights.
- **Stay hydrated:** Bring a refillable bottle and drink water regularly. Our [carry-on packing essentials](/travel/carry-on-packing-essentials/) list includes a few good ones.
- **Pack a spare pair:** A fresh pair for the return flight makes a long trip more comfortable.
- **Ask your doctor first** if you have circulation problems, diabetes or a history of blood clots.
