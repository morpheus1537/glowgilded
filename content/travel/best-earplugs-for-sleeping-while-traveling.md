## How to choose travel earplugs for sleep

1. **Noise rating:** NRR (US) and SNR (EU) show how much noise a plug can block. Higher numbers block more.
2. **Material:** Foam blocks the most noise. Moldable silicone and wax cover the ear opening and suit side sleepers. Reusable silicone tips last longer.
3. **Fit:** Small ears may find foam plugs too big. Moldable or multi-size options help.
4. **Sleeping position:** Side sleepers press their ear into a pillow, so low-profile plugs are more comfortable.
5. **Reusability:** Disposable plugs are hygienic for travel; reusable ones cut waste.
6. **Packing:** A small case keeps plugs clean in a bag.

## Tips for sleeping better on the road

- **Insert foam plugs correctly:** Roll tightly, pull the top of your ear up and back, insert and hold while it expands.
- **Pair with a mask:** Blocking light and noise together works better than either alone.
- **Use white noise:** A fan app or hotel AC can mask sounds earplugs do not fully block.
- **Set a loud alarm:** Earplugs can make alarms harder to hear, so use vibration or a louder tone.
- **Keep them clean:** Store plugs in a case and wash hands before inserting.
- **Pack spares:** Foam plugs are easy to lose on planes, so bring extra pairs.

## Earplugs and long flights

On a long-haul flight, cabin noise sits around a constant hum for hours, which is tiring even if you are not trying to sleep. Earplugs reduce that fatigue, and silicone options like Mack's Pillow Soft can also help with pressure during descent.
