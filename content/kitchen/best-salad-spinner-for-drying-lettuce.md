## How to choose a salad spinner

1. **Size:** Small spinners suit herbs and one or two people. Family-size models around 6 qt hold several heads of lettuce.
2. **Mechanism:** Pump spinners are fast and one-handed. Crank spinners give finer speed control.
3. **Brake:** A brake button stops the basket instantly so you can unload without waiting.
4. **Bowl material:** Plastic is light, glass doubles as a serving bowl, and stainless steel is durable and dishwasher friendly.
5. **Cleaning:** Look for a lid that comes apart so water does not get trapped inside.
6. **Storage:** A pump that locks flat makes the spinner easier to stack in a cabinet.

## Tips for drier, crisper greens

- **Rinse in the basket:** Fill the bowl with cold water, swish the greens, lift the basket out and pour off the water before spinning.
- **Do not overfill:** Spin in batches so water can escape from every leaf.
- **Spin twice:** Pour off water after the first spin, then spin again for the driest leaves.
- **Tear before or after:** Tearing large leaves first helps them dry evenly.
- **Store smart:** Wrap dry greens loosely in a towel in a container in the fridge. Our [glass food storage containers](/kitchen/best-glass-food-storage-containers-for-leftovers/) guide has good options.
- **Dry the spinner too:** Take the lid apart and let parts air-dry so moisture does not build up inside.

## Do you really need a salad spinner?

You can pat greens dry with towels, but it takes longer, uses more paper and rarely gets leaves fully dry. If you eat salad a few times a week, wash herbs or prep vegetables in batches, a spinner pays for itself in convenience. If you only buy prewashed greens, a small model like the OXO Little is plenty for herbs.
