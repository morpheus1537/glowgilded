## How to choose a kitchen scale for baking

1. **Capacity:** An 11 lb (5 kg) scale covers most recipes. Bread bakers and big-batch cooks may want 22 or 33 lb.
2. **Precision:** Look for 1 gram increments, which every scale here offers.
3. **Units:** Grams are the baker's standard. Ounces, pounds and ml or fluid ounce modes are handy for recipes written in other units.
4. **Tare function:** Essential for weighing ingredients into a bowl. All five have it.
5. **Readable display:** A backlit screen, or a display that sits beyond the platform, stays visible under a big mixing bowl.
6. **Power:** Most use AAA batteries. A rechargeable model saves buying batteries.
7. **Easy cleaning:** A stainless steel top or sealed controls wipe clean after a flour spill.

## Baking scale tips

- **Weigh in grams:** Grams are more precise than ounces and easiest for scaling recipes up or down.
- **Tare between ingredients:** Add flour, tare, add sugar, tare, and you can mix in one bowl.
- **Use a flat, hard surface:** Scales can read inaccurately on a towel or an uneven counter.
- **Place the bowl first:** Put your bowl on the scale before turning it on or pressing tare.
- **Replace weak batteries:** Odd readings are often a sign the batteries are low.
- **Pair with good tools:** A scale works best with sturdy bowls and pans, and our [Dutch oven picks for bread baking](/kitchen/best-dutch-oven-for-bread-baking/) are a natural match for the 22 lb Greater Goods.
