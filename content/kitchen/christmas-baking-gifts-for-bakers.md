## How to choose a baking gift

- **Match the gift to what they bake most.** Cake people will love the Bundt pan or turntable, while pie and cookie bakers will use a French rolling pin every week in December.
- **Upgrade a tool they already use.** Most bakers own a rolling pin and some kind of cake pan; a better version of a daily tool is more welcome than a gadget for a recipe they never make.
- **Think about skill level.** The Bundt pan makes a beautiful cake with no decorating skills. The turntable kit suits someone who wants to learn, and the French pin rewards a little practice.
- **Check storage space.** A rolling pin fits in a drawer, but a 12-inch Bundt pan and an 11-inch turntable need cabinet space.
- **Read the care instructions.** Wood should be wiped clean, not soaked. For nonstick bakeware, gentle washing with a soft sponge helps protect the coating.

## Holiday baking tips for using these gifts

- **Grease Bundt pans carefully.** Brush softened butter or shortening into every flute and dust with flour, or use a baking spray with flour, then let the cake cool in the pan for about 10 to 15 minutes before turning it out.
- **Chill dough before rolling.** Cold pie and cookie dough sticks less and holds its shape. Roll from the center outward and give the dough a quarter turn every few strokes.
- **Crumb coat layer cakes.** Spread a thin first layer of frosting on the turntable, chill the cake for 15 to 20 minutes, then add the final coat and smooth it with the straight scraper as you spin.
