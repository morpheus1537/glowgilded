## How to choose a nonstick pan for eggs

1. **Size:** 8 inches suits one or two eggs. 9.5 to 10 inches suits omelets and larger scrambles.
2. **Stovetop:** Induction cooktops need a magnetic base. Check before you buy, because two pans here do not work on induction.
3. **Coating:** Traditional nonstick and ceramic both release eggs well. Check the listing for what the coating is free of, such as PFOA.
4. **Oven use:** If you like to finish frittatas or omelets in the oven, check the oven-safe temperature.
5. **Handle:** A cool-touch handle makes flipping easier. A hanging hole helps with storage.
6. **Cleaning:** Most nonstick pans wipe clean, and some list dishwasher safety, though hand washing is usually gentler on the coating.

## Tips for perfect eggs in a nonstick pan

- **Keep the heat low to medium:** Eggs cook best gently, and high heat shortens the life of most nonstick coatings.
- **Use a little fat:** A small pat of butter or a drizzle of oil adds flavor and helps eggs release cleanly.
- **Preheat briefly:** Let the pan warm for a minute before adding butter, but never heat an empty nonstick pan on high.
- **Use soft tools:** A silicone spatula or wooden spoon protects the coating, unless your pan is metal utensil safe.
- **Low and slow for scrambles:** Stir gently on low heat and pull the eggs off just before they look done.
- **Cool before washing:** Plunging a hot pan into cold water can warp it, so let it cool first.

## How to make a nonstick pan last longer

Nonstick coatings wear out over time, but good habits stretch their life. Hand wash with a soft sponge even if the pan is listed as dishwasher safe, because harsh detergents and high heat can dull the surface. Avoid cooking sprays, which can leave a sticky residue, and store pans with a cloth or pan protector between them so they do not scratch each other. When eggs begin to stick even with fat in the pan, or the coating looks scratched or flaky, it is time to replace it.
