Marcato has made pasta tools since 1930, and this one is made and assembled in its factory in Italy from chrome-plated steel. You clamp it to the counter, feed dough through the smooth rollers, and turn the dial to roll it thinner on each pass.

Out of the box it makes three shapes: lasagna sheets (Marcato lists 0.5 mm to 4.8 mm thick), fettuccine (6 mm) and tagliolini (1.5 mm). Sheets come out up to 150 mm, about 6 inches, wide. Marcato sells about a dozen extra cutter attachments and a Pastadrive motor separately, so the gift can grow with the cook. With 27,000+ ratings and a 4.6-star average, it is one of the most reviewed pasta makers on Amazon.

The pasta lover who already cooks a lot and wants a real upgrade, couples who like cooking together, and anyone who has talked about "learning to make pasta one day." It is also a strong wedding or housewarming gift that lasts for years.

## Best for filled pasta: Bellemain Ravioli Mold & Press

### Why we like it

Ravioli is where many new pasta makers give up, because cutting and pinching every edge takes a long time. The Bellemain kit has two parts: a metal cutting frame and a plastic mold with a textured, stick-resistant surface. You lay a pasta sheet on the frame and press it in with the mold to make 12 pockets. Then you spoon in the filling, lay a second sheet on top and roll over it with a rolling pin. The crimped edges of the frame cut and seal all 12 ravioli at once.

The listing says the metal frame is solid-cast and about 1/8 inch thick, so it will not bend. With a 4.6-star average from more than 6,000 ratings, it is one of the best-reviewed ravioli makers on Amazon. It also works for pelmeni, pierogi-style dumplings and small hand pies.

### Who it's for

Anyone who loves ravioli or tortellini, families who like making dinner together, and the pasta machine owner who is ready for filled shapes. It pairs perfectly with the Atlas 150, which rolls the thin, even sheets this mold needs.

### Pros

- Shapes, cuts and seals 12 ravioli in one go
- Simple two-piece design with nothing to assemble
- Rigid metal frame and a mold made to resist sticking
- Small and affordable enough to be a stocking stuffer or add-on gift

## How to choose a gift for a pasta lover

- **Start with what they already own.** If they have no pasta tools, the pasta machine is the gift that changes how they cook. If they already have a machine, the ravioli mold and drying rack fill the gaps.
- **Think about the shapes they love.** Fans of long noodles will use the drying rack every time. Anyone who orders ravioli or tortellini at restaurants will get the most from the ravioli mold.
- **Check their counter.** A clamp-on pasta machine needs a sturdy counter or table edge with a lip the clamp can grip. Check that their counter has one, or plan to use a sturdy wooden table.
- **Bundle for a bigger moment.** All three tools together make a complete "pasta night" kit. Add a bag of 00 flour or semolina, a wedge of parmesan and a handwritten recipe card for a gift that feels personal.

## Tips for the first homemade pasta night

- **Rest the dough.** Wrap egg pasta dough and let it rest at room temperature for about 30 minutes before rolling. It rolls out smoother and tears less.
- **Roll gradually.** Start at the widest setting, fold the sheet and run it through a few times, then move one setting thinner on each pass instead of jumping ahead.
- **Use semolina for dusting.** A little semolina or flour on sheets, cut noodles and the ravioli frame keeps everything from sticking.
- **Cook fresh pasta briefly.** Fresh noodles often cook in just 2 to 4 minutes in well-salted boiling water, so taste early.
