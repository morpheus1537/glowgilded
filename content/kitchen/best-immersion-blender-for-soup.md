## How to choose an immersion blender for soup

1. **Shaft length:** A longer arm, like KitchenAid's 8-inch arm, reaches the bottom of tall stockpots without the motor dipping into the soup.
2. **Speed control:** A variable trigger or pressure-sensitive button lets you start slow and avoid splatter.
3. **Pot protection:** Pan guards and nylon blade guards help protect nonstick and enameled pots.
4. **Power:** 300 watts handles soft cooked vegetables, while more power blends faster and copes with tougher ingredients.
5. **Splash and suction control:** Designs that reduce suction keep the blender from sticking to the bottom of the pot.
6. **Attachments and cleanup:** Look for a detachable, dishwasher-safe shaft and any jars, whisks or choppers you will actually use.

## Tips for blending soup safely

- **Take the pot off the heat:** Let the soup stop bubbling before you blend.
- **Submerge first, then switch on:** Starting with the blade under the surface prevents splatter.
- **Tilt the pot:** For shallow amounts, tilt the pot so the blade stays covered.
- **Start low:** Begin on low speed, then increase once the soup starts to smooth out.
- **Move slowly:** Draw the blender up and down and around the pot rather than holding it still.
- **Rest the motor:** Follow each listing's guidance on run times, especially with tough vegetables.
- **For chunky add-ins:** Chop vegetables first with a [mini food processor](/kitchen/best-mini-food-processor-for-chopping/), then blend only part of the soup for a rustic texture.
