## How to choose a slow cooker for a busy family

1. **Capacity:** 6 to 7 quarts suits most families of four to six; 8 quarts or more is for big families, batch cooking and holidays.
2. **Programmable or manual:** Programmable models count down cook time and switch to warm on their own; manual dials are simpler but need you to switch them.
3. **Keep warm:** A warm setting holds food at serving temperature for late arrivals.
4. **Lid:** Locking lids help when you carry food to potlucks and parties.
5. **Extra functions:** Multi-cookers can sear, braise or cook rice in the same pot.
6. **Cleanup:** Look for dishwasher-safe crocks and lids.

## Slow cooker tips for busy weeknights

- **Prep the night before:** Chop vegetables and trim meat in the evening, store them in the fridge, and load the crock in the morning.
- **Fill it right:** Most slow cookers work best between half and two-thirds full; check your manual.
- **Keep the lid on:** Lifting the lid lets heat escape and can add cooking time.
- **Layer smart:** Put dense root vegetables at the bottom, closer to the heat, and meat on top.
- **Use low for all-day cooking:** Low is usually best for long workdays; high suits shorter cook times.
- **Brown for flavor:** If your cooker cannot sear, brown meat in a skillet first; a cast iron skillet works well for this.
- **Food safety:** Thaw frozen meat in the fridge before slow cooking, and follow USDA guidance on safe temperatures.

## Easy family slow cooker ideas

Chili, beef stew, pulled pork, chicken tortilla soup, pot roast with carrots and potatoes, and lentil soup are all crowd-pleasers that need little hands-on time. Make a double batch in a 7- or 8-quart cooker and freeze half for another busy night. For breakfast, try a slow cooker oatmeal or egg casserole, following a tested recipe for your model's size.
