How to choose a knife sharpener

  1. Manual or electric: Manual pull-through sharpeners are compact and quiet. Electric sharpeners work faster and can reshape very dull edges.
  2. Number of stages: A coarse stage repairs dull or chipped edges, a fine stage polishes, and some sharpeners add a third repair or stropping stage.
  3. Edge angle: Many Western knives have a 20-degree edge. Some electric sharpeners can convert knives to a sharper 15-degree edge, so check your knife maker's advice first.
  4. Serrated knives: If you want to sharpen bread knives, choose a sharpener that lists serrated blades.
  5. Stability and safety: A non-slip base, a finger guard or an included cut-resistant glove makes sharpening safer.
  6. Storage: Compact sharpeners fit in a utensil drawer, while electric models need counter or cabinet space.

Knife sharpening tips

  • Start coarse only when needed: Use the coarse slot for truly dull knives, then switch to the fine slot for regular touch-ups.
  • Light pressure: Let the sharpener do the work. Pressing hard removes more metal than you need.
  • Same number of passes: Pull through the same number of times each time for an even edge.
  • Check your knife maker's angle: Some Japanese knives use different angles, so check before using a preset sharpener.
  • Wipe the blade: Clean off metal particles after sharpening before you cut food.
  • Store knives properly: A block or guard keeps edges sharp longer. Sharp knives also make holiday jobs easier, though an electric carving knife is handy for big roasts.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest knife sharpener to use at home?

Pull-through sharpeners with preset angles, like the KitchenIQ Edge Grip, Kitchellence 3-stage or WÜSTHOF 2-stage, are the easiest. You simply draw the blade through each slot a few times, with no angle to hold yourself.

Are electric knife sharpeners better than manual ones?

Electric sharpeners like the Chef'sChoice 15XV are faster at reshaping very dull edges and can convert knives to a sharper 15-degree edge. Manual pull-through sharpeners are smaller, quieter and need no outlet, and they are fine for regular touch-ups.

Can you sharpen serrated knives with these sharpeners?

Several can. KitchenIQ lists its Edge Grip for straight and serrated knives, Chef'sChoice says the 15XV handles straight edge and serrated blades, and AccuSharp lists serrated knives too. Check each listing before sharpening serrated blades.

How often should you sharpen kitchen knives?

Most home cooks only need a full sharpening a few times a year, with quick honing or fine-stage touch-ups between. If a knife slides off a tomato skin instead of slicing it, it is time to sharpen.

What is the difference between the coarse and fine slots?

The coarse slot, usually carbide or diamond, removes metal to reshape a dull or damaged edge. The fine slot, usually ceramic, polishes and refines the edge and is the one to use for quick touch-ups.
