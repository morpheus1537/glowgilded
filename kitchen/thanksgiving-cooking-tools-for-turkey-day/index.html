Turkey day timeline tips

  • Thaw early: a frozen turkey thaws in the fridge at roughly one day per 4 to 5 pounds, so a big bird needs to go in the fridge several days ahead.
  • Do a dry run: put the empty roasting pan in your oven a week before to check the fit, with the rack in place.
  • Start checking early: begin testing the temperature well before the recipe's estimated finish time. Test the thickest part of the breast, the thigh and the wing.
  • Rest the bird: let the turkey rest before carving. That frees up the oven for sides and gives you time to make the gravy.
  • Make gravy in the pan: set the roasting pan across two burners, deglaze, then strain and separate the drippings.
  • Mash and whip last: potatoes and whipped cream are best made right before serving, which is when a hand mixer earns its spot on the counter.

What to look for in Thanksgiving tools

  • Size: measure your oven, your largest platter and your storage space before buying a roasting pan.
  • Speed and accuracy: an instant-read thermometer should settle in a few seconds. The faster it is, the less heat you lose with the door open.
  • Straining: a fat separator with a built-in strainer saves you from juggling a sieve and a cup.
  • Year-round use: each tool here is useful well beyond November, which makes it easier to justify the space.

Frequently asked questions

What tools do I need to cook a Thanksgiving turkey?

At minimum, a sturdy roasting pan with a rack, an instant-read meat thermometer and a carving knife. A fat separator makes better gravy, and a hand mixer handles mashed potatoes, whipped cream and pie fillings on the same day.

What temperature should a turkey be when it is done?

USDA guidance says turkey is safe at 165°F, measured in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost part of the thigh and the innermost part of the wing. Check more than one spot, and let the bird rest before carving.

How big a roasting pan do I need for a turkey?

A 16-inch roasting pan handles most family-size turkeys. Before you buy, measure your oven to make sure the pan, including the handles, fits with the door closed and leaves some space around it for air to move.

Why should I use a rack in the roasting pan?

A rack lifts the turkey off the bottom of the pan so hot air can reach the underside and the skin browns more evenly. It also keeps the bird out of the drippings, which you can then use for gravy.

How does a fat separator work?

You pour the pan juices into the cup and wait a few minutes. The fat rises to the top, and because the spout draws from the bottom, you can pour off the flavorful juices for gravy while leaving most of the fat behind.
