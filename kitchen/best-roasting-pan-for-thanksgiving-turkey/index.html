How to choose a roasting pan

  1. Size: Measure your oven. A 16- or 17-inch pan suits most holiday turkeys, while a 13-inch pan fits chickens and turkey breasts.
  2. Rack type: V-shaped racks cradle a whole bird; flat or U-shaped racks also work for roasts and vegetables.
  3. Material: Tri-ply stainless steel is durable and stovetop friendly; nonstick carbon steel is lighter and easier to clean; enameled steel suits covered roasting.
  4. Handles: Wide, sturdy handles matter when you lift a hot, heavy pan with oven mitts.
  5. Oven-safe temperature: Check the limit if you like to start at high heat.

Turkey roasting tips

  • Measure before you buy: Check your oven's interior width, including room for the handles.
  • Thaw early: A frozen turkey needs about one day in the fridge for every four to five pounds.
  • Use a thermometer: Cook until the thickest part of the thigh and breast reach a safe 165°F.
  • Let it rest: Rest the turkey for 20 to 30 minutes before carving so the juices settle. Our electric carving knife picks make slicing quick.
  • Save the drippings: Pour them into a fat separator or saucepan for gravy.

Frequently asked questions

What size roasting pan do I need for a turkey?

A pan around 16 by 13 inches handles the turkeys most families buy for Thanksgiving, as long as there is a little space around the bird for air to circulate. Measure your oven first; wide handles add inches, and a pan that is too big will block airflow.

Do I need a rack in my roasting pan?

A rack lifts the turkey off the bottom so hot air reaches the underside and the skin browns more evenly, and it keeps the bird out of the drippings. A V-shaped rack cradles the bird, while a flat or U-shaped rack works for roasts and vegetables too.

Is stainless steel or nonstick better for roasting a turkey?

Stainless steel builds browned bits for gravy and can often go on the stovetop, but needs more scrubbing. Nonstick releases food easily and cleans up quickly, but usually has a lower oven-safe limit and should not be used with sharp metal tools unless the maker says so.

Can I use a roasting pan on the stovetop to make gravy?

Only if the maker says it is stovetop safe. The Cuisinart MultiClad Pro is listed as compatible with induction, gas, electric and glass-ceramic cooktops. For other pans, pour the drippings into a saucepan instead.

Can I roast a turkey in a lidded roaster?

Yes. A covered roaster traps steam to keep meat moist, and you can remove the lid near the end to brown the skin. Check the capacity; the 13-inch Granite Ware roaster is listed for up to 7 pounds of poultry, so it suits a small bird or turkey breast.
