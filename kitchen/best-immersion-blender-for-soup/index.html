How to choose an immersion blender for soup

  1. Shaft length: A longer arm, like KitchenAid's 8-inch arm, reaches the bottom of tall stockpots without the motor dipping into the soup.
  2. Speed control: A variable trigger or pressure-sensitive button lets you start slow and avoid splatter.
  3. Pot protection: Pan guards and nylon blade guards help protect nonstick and enameled pots.
  4. Power: 300 watts handles soft cooked vegetables, while more power blends faster and copes with tougher ingredients.
  5. Splash and suction control: Designs that reduce suction keep the blender from sticking to the bottom of the pot.
  6. Attachments and cleanup: Look for a detachable, dishwasher-safe shaft and any jars, whisks or choppers you will actually use.

Tips for blending soup safely

  • Take the pot off the heat: Let the soup stop bubbling before you blend.
  • Submerge first, then switch on: Starting with the blade under the surface prevents splatter.
  • Tilt the pot: For shallow amounts, tilt the pot so the blade stays covered.
  • Start low: Begin on low speed, then increase once the soup starts to smooth out.
  • Move slowly: Draw the blender up and down and around the pot rather than holding it still.
  • Rest the motor: Follow each listing's guidance on run times, especially with tough vegetables.
  • For chunky add-ins: Chop vegetables first with a mini food processor, then blend only part of the soup for a rustic texture.

Frequently asked questions

Can you use an immersion blender in a hot pot of soup?

Yes, blending soup right in the pot is the main reason to own one. Take the pot off the heat, keep the blade fully submerged before you switch on, and start on low speed to avoid splatters. Turelar specifically lists its blender as safe with hot ingredients.

Will an immersion blender scratch my pots?

A bare metal blade guard can scratch nonstick coatings if it bumps the bottom. KitchenAid includes a removable pan guard and Turelar uses a food-grade nylon blade guard designed to prevent scratches.

How many watts do you need for an immersion blender for soup?

Soft cooked vegetables puree easily, so even the 300-watt OVENTE and Bonsenkitchen handle most soups. Higher-powered models like the 500-watt Braun or 1,100-watt Turelar blend faster and handle tougher or more fibrous ingredients.

Is an immersion blender better than a regular blender for soup?

For soup, an immersion blender is usually easier and safer because you blend in the pot instead of transferring hot liquid into a pitcher in batches. A countertop blender can make an even silkier texture but means more steps and cleanup.

Are immersion blender parts dishwasher safe?

Usually only the detachable blending shaft or arm is. KitchenAid and OVENTE list dishwasher-safe arms or shafts, and Bonsenkitchen says its blades are dishwasher safe, but the motor body should only be wiped clean.
