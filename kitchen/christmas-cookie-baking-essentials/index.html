How to bake Christmas cookies like a pro

  1. Chill the dough. Most cut-out and drop cookies hold their shape better after an hour in the fridge.
  2. Scoop evenly. Use the same scoop for every cookie on a tray.
  3. Line the pan. A silicone mat stops sticking and keeps bottoms from over-browning.
  4. Rotate trays. Turn the pan halfway through baking for even color.
  5. Cool completely. Move cookies to a rack before icing, or the icing will melt.
  6. Decorate in stages. Outline, let set, then flood and add details.

Buying tips

Frequently asked questions

What do I need to bake Christmas cookies?

The core kit is a good sheet pan, a non-stick liner such as a silicone baking mat, a cookie scoop for even portions, a cooling rack, and piping bags and tips for decorating. A stand mixer makes big batches much easier but is optional.

Are silicone baking mats better than parchment paper?

They are reusable, which is the main advantage. The Amazon Basics mats release food without oil, spray or parchment, are oven-safe up to 480°F and are dishwasher safe, according to the listing. Always set them on a baking tray rather than directly on the oven rack.

What size cookie scoop should I use?

A 1 tablespoon (#60) scoop makes small cookies, a 2 tablespoon (#40) scoop makes standard cookies and a 3 tablespoon (#20) scoop makes large bakery-style cookies. The HOMURE H set includes all three.

How many cookies can a KitchenAid Artisan mix at once?

KitchenAid says its 5-quart bowl can mix up to 9 dozen cookies in a single batch, based on 28 g of dough per cookie using the flat beater.

How do you decorate Christmas cookies with royal icing?

Outline each cookie with a small round piping tip, let it set for a few minutes, then flood the center with thinner icing. A piping kit with disposable bags, couplers and several tips, like the Riccle set, lets you switch colors quickly.
