How to carve a turkey with an electric knife

  1. Rest the bird. Let the turkey rest loosely covered so the juices settle before carving.
  2. Set up a stable board. Use a large board with a groove for juices, and put a damp towel underneath so it does not slide.
  3. Remove legs and wings. Pull each leg away from the body and cut through the joint. Do the same with the wings.
  4. Take off each breast. Cut down along the breastbone, then follow the ribcage to remove the whole breast in one piece.
  5. Slice across the grain. Lay the breast flat and slice it crosswise with the electric knife, using light pressure and letting the blades move.
  6. Arrange and serve. Fan slices on a warm platter with the dark meat alongside.

Buying tips

Frequently asked questions

Is an electric knife better for carving turkey?

For many home cooks, yes. Two serrated blades move back and forth quickly, so you guide the knife instead of sawing. That makes it easier to cut thin, even slices of breast meat, especially if you only carve a big bird once or twice a year.

How do you carve a turkey with an electric knife?

Let the turkey rest first, then remove the legs and wings with a regular knife or the electric knife at the joints. Hold the bird steady with a carving fork and slice the breast with light downward pressure, letting the blades do the work rather than pushing hard.

Can you put electric knife blades in the dishwasher?

It depends on the model. The BLACK+DECKER listing says its blades are dishwasher safe, and the Cuisinart set also mentions dishwasher-safe cleanup. Check the manual for your knife, and always unplug it and remove the blades carefully before cleaning.

Can an electric knife cut bread?

Yes. All four knives in this guide list bread as well as meat, and the serrated blades are useful for crusty loaves and homemade bread that a straight knife can crush.

How long should a turkey rest before carving?

Many cooks rest a roast turkey for about 20 to 30 minutes, loosely covered, so the juices settle before slicing. Follow your recipe and food-safety guidance for checking doneness.
