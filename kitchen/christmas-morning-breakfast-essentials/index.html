Christmas morning breakfast game plan

  1. The night before: Mix pancake and waffle batter, fill the dispenser and refrigerate. Set out plates, syrup and toppings.
  2. Wake up: Preheat the griddle while coffee brews. Start the waffle maker at a separate station.
  3. Cook in waves: Pancakes and bacon on the griddle, waffles on the side. Keep finished food warm in a low oven.
  4. Toppings bar: Berries, whipped cream and syrup let everyone build their own plate while you keep cooking.

  • Match the tool to the crowd: Large group, choose the griddle. Small household, the Cuisinart combo might be all you need.
  • Store smart: Mini waffle makers and batter dispensers fit in a drawer; big griddles need a cabinet or shelf.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest Christmas morning breakfast for a crowd?

Pancakes on a large electric griddle are the easiest, because you can cook a full batch at once and keep the stove free. Mix the batter the night before and refrigerate it, then pour straight from a batter dispenser in the morning.

Is an electric griddle better than a stovetop pan for pancakes?

For a crowd, usually yes. A 22-inch griddle like the Presto holds far more pancakes than a skillet, and its heat control keeps the temperature steady so batches cook evenly.

Can I make waffles ahead for Christmas morning?

Yes. Cook waffles the day before, cool them on a rack, then refrigerate or freeze and re-crisp them in a toaster or oven. Fresh is best, but make-ahead waffles take pressure off a busy morning.

Are mini waffle makers worth it?

They are worth it if you like small portions, kid-friendly breakfasts or making waffles one at a time. For a large family that wants full-size waffles fast, a Belgian waffle maker is the better choice.

How do I keep pancakes warm while I cook the rest?

Put finished pancakes on a sheet pan in an oven set to a low warm setting, in a single layer or loosely stacked, so they stay warm without steaming and turning soggy.
