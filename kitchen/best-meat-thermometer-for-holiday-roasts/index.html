Instant-read vs. leave-in thermometers

  • Instant-read: You insert it, read the number in a second or two and pull it out. Great for checking several spots and for everyday cooking.
  • Leave-in probe: A probe stays in the roast with a cable or wirelessly and alerts you when it reaches your target. Great for long roasts where you do not want to keep opening the oven.
  • Both: Many holiday cooks use a leave-in probe to track progress and an instant-read to double-check before carving.

Holiday roast tips

  • Know your targets: USDA guidance is 165°F for poultry and 145°F for whole cuts of beef, pork and lamb, followed by a rest. Many cooks pull beef roasts earlier for rarer doneness.
  • Plan for carryover: Big roasts keep rising a few degrees as they rest, so pull them a little before your final target.
  • Check the thickest part: Avoid bone and fat pockets, which can throw off readings.
  • Rest before carving: Resting lets juices settle. Then slice with a good knife; see our best electric carving knives for turkey.
  • Use the right pan: A sturdy rack keeps the bottom from stewing. See our best roasting pans for Thanksgiving turkey.
  • Test accuracy before the big day: An ice-water bath should read about 32°F.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of meat thermometer is best for a turkey?

Many cooks use two. A leave-in probe thermometer like the TempPro TempTrack or MEATER Plus tracks the bird while it roasts, and an instant-read thermometer like the Alpha Grillers checks the thickest part of the thigh and breast before you pull it out.

Where do you put the thermometer in a turkey?

Insert it into the thickest part of the thigh without touching bone, and check the thickest part of the breast too. USDA guidance is 165°F for poultry, and bone contact can give a misleading reading.

Can you leave an instant-read thermometer in the oven?

No. Instant-read thermometers like the Alpha Grillers, Javelin PRO and TempSwift are made for quick spot checks. For in-oven monitoring, use a probe thermometer with a cable or a wireless probe made for oven use.

How accurate does a meat thermometer need to be?

For holiday roasts, a degree or two matters most near the end of cooking. Lavatools lists ±0.5°F with laboratory calibration, TempSwift lists ±0.9°F and TempTrack lists ±1.8°F, all close enough for home cooking.

Do I need to calibrate my meat thermometer?

It is worth checking once in a while. Alpha Grillers and TempSwift both include a calibration feature, and a quick test in ice water should read about 32°F.
