Winter care tips for indoor cats

  • Block drafts. Move beds away from exterior doors and cold tile floors, or raise them off the ground.
  • Follow the sun. Put a perch or mat where the sun lands in the afternoon.
  • Keep water fresh. Indoor heating dries the air, so make sure water is always available.
  • Play every day. Ten to fifteen minutes of active play helps prevent winter weight gain and boredom.
  • Brush more often. Winter coats shed and mat, and grooming improves insulation.
  • Watch the space heater. Keep cats away from space heaters and fireplaces, and never leave them unattended.
  • Check the car. Outdoor and community cats sometimes sleep under car hoods for warmth; tap the hood before starting the engine.

Frequently asked questions

How can I keep my indoor cat warm in winter?

Give your cat a warm place to sleep away from drafts, such as a heated or self-warming bed, and a sunny window perch to soak up daylight. Keep beds off cold tile floors and away from doors that open to the outside.

Are heated cat beds safe?

The K&H Thermo-Kitty Bed uses a low 4-watt heater with a pet-activated thermostat that warms the bed only when your cat is in it, and it is safety listed. As with any electric pet product, check the cord regularly and don't use it with cats that chew cords.

Do self-warming cat beds really work?

Self-warming beds don't produce heat on their own. They reflect and hold your cat's body heat. The Mora Pets mat uses a carbon-infused cover and a noiseless Mylar layer to do this, with no cord or electricity needed.

How do I keep my cat active in winter?

Short daily play sessions, a tunnel to run through, and a cat tree to climb and scratch help indoor cats burn energy when it's too cold or dark to see much outside. Rotating toys keeps things interesting.

What size heated cat bed should I get?

K&H says the small 16-inch Thermo-Kitty Bed suits pets under 15 pounds and the large 20-inch bed, linked in this guide, suits pets over 15 pounds.
