How to choose a no-pull harness

  1. Leash points: A front chest clip discourages pulling; a back clip is better for relaxed walks. Two is most flexible.
  2. Fit and adjustability: More adjustment points give a better fit for deep-chested or growing dogs.
  3. Padding: Padded straps and mesh help prevent rubbing on long walks.
  4. Ease of use: Step-in or buckle designs that do not go over the head suit head-shy dogs.
  5. Visibility: Reflective strips or stitching matter on dark winter evenings.
  6. Handle: A top handle helps with control and lifting dogs into the car.

Tips for training with a no-pull harness

  • Measure first: Use a soft tape around the neck and widest part of the chest, then compare with the size chart.
  • Check the fit: Two fingers should slide under each strap. Readjust after a few walks as straps settle.
  • Reward loose leash walking: Treat and praise your dog when the leash is slack; a harness works best alongside training.
  • Stop when they pull: Pause until your dog comes back toward you, then continue.
  • Keep walks short at first: New harnesses feel strange; build up gradually.
  • Watch for rubbing: Check behind the front legs and under the chest for redness.
  • Keep nails trimmed: Good traction helps walks; see our best dog nail grinder for anxious dogs.

Front clip vs back clip

A front clip sits on the chest and turns your dog sideways when they pull, which makes it the better choice for training and strong pullers. A back clip sits between the shoulders and is comfortable for calm walks and running, but it can encourage pulling because dogs lean into it. Harnesses with both, like the PHOEPET, PoyPet and AUROTH, let you switch as your dog's leash manners improve.

Frequently asked questions

Do front-clip harnesses really stop pulling?

They usually reduce pulling rather than stop it. When the dog pulls, a front chest clip turns them back toward you, which takes away the forward momentum; pairing it with reward-based leash training works best.

Should I clip the leash to the front or back of a no-pull harness?

Use the front clip when your dog tends to pull or during training walks, and the back clip for relaxed walks or running. PHOEPET, PoyPet and AUROTH all have both front and back attachment points.

How should a no-pull harness fit?

Snug but not tight. You should be able to slide two fingers under the straps, and the harness should not rub behind the front legs. Always measure your dog's neck and chest before ordering.

Is a harness better than a collar for dogs that pull?

Many owners prefer a harness for pullers because it spreads pressure across the chest instead of the throat. PetSafe says the Easy Walk rests across the chest to avoid gagging and choking.

Can my dog wear a no-pull harness all day?

It is best to take the harness off after walks so the straps do not rub or trap moisture. Check the fit regularly, especially for growing puppies.
