How to choose interactive toys for an indoor cat

  1. Play style: Some cats chase, some pounce, some wrestle. Watch what your cat does and choose a toy that matches.
  2. With you or solo: Wand toys need a person. Automatic toys and puzzles keep cats busy when you are working or away.
  3. Safety: Avoid small parts that can be chewed off, and always supervise string toys.
  4. Power: Automatic toys run on batteries or USB. Rechargeable or plug-in options save on batteries.
  5. Noise: Timid cats may avoid loud motors, so look for quiet operation.
  6. Variety: Rotating two or three types of toys keeps play interesting.

Tips for better playtime

  • Move toys like prey: Drag the wand away from your cat and let it hide behind furniture instead of dangling it in their face.
  • Let them win: Finish each session with a catch, then offer a treat or a meal.
  • Rotate toys: Put some away for a week and bring them back to keep them exciting.
  • Play before bed: An evening play session can help night-active cats settle down.
  • Store strings safely: Put wand toys away after play.
  • Check for wear: Replace toys with loose parts or torn seams.

Frequently asked questions

How much playtime does an indoor cat need?

Many cats do well with two or three short play sessions a day of about 10 to 15 minutes each. Active young cats may want more, while older cats may prefer shorter, gentler sessions.

What toys keep indoor cats entertained when you are not home?

Automatic toys that start on their own, such as the Potaroma 3-in-1 or ORSDA laser toy, and treat puzzles like the Nina Ottosson Buggin' Out can keep cats busy. Wand toys with strings should only be used with supervision.

Are laser toys bad for cats?

Laser toys are fine in moderation, but some cats get frustrated when they never catch anything. End sessions with a toy they can grab, like a kicker, or a treat so play feels rewarding.

Are wand toys safe to leave out?

No. Strings can be dangerous if swallowed, and the MeoHui listing says to supervise your cat during play and store the wand where pets cannot reach it.

How do you get a lazy cat to play?

Try short sessions around dawn or dusk when cats are naturally more active, move the toy like prey (away from the cat, hiding behind furniture), and rotate toys so they stay novel. Catnip toys can also spark interest.
