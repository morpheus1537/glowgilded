How to choose a scratching post

  1. Height: Look for a post tall enough for a full stretch. Around 32 to 34 inches suits most adult cats.
  2. Stability: A wide, heavy base stops the post from tipping. A wobbly post can scare a cat away for good.
  3. Material: Woven sisal and sisal rope are tough and satisfying. Corrugated cardboard suits cats who like to shred.
  4. Vertical or horizontal: Some cats scratch upward, others prefer the floor. An L-shaped scratcher offers both.
  5. Toys: A dangling ball or mouse can lure a curious cat to the post.
  6. Post thickness: Large cats benefit from a thicker post they can wrap their paws around.

Tips to get your cat to use a scratching post

  • Place it by the problem spot: Put the new post right next to the furniture your cat already scratches.
  • Use catnip or treats: Rub a little catnip on the post or reward your cat when it scratches there.
  • Play near it: Drag a wand toy up the post so your cat's claws sink into the sisal.
  • Make the couch less appealing: Cover favorite scratching spots with a throw while your cat learns.
  • Never force paws onto the post: Let your cat discover it on its own terms.

Frequently asked questions

How tall should a scratching post be for an indoor cat?

Choose a post tall enough for your cat to stretch fully while standing on its back legs, usually around 30 to 34 inches for an adult cat. The vertical posts in this guide are 32 to 34 inches tall.

Why does my cat scratch the couch instead of the scratching post?

Cats often skip posts that wobble, are too short, or sit in an out-of-the-way corner. Put a sturdy post right next to the furniture your cat scratches, and reward your cat with treats or play when it uses the post.

Is sisal or cardboard better for cats?

Many cats love sisal because it is tough and satisfying to shred, while others prefer corrugated cardboard. If you are not sure, offer one of each, such as a tall sisal post and an L-shaped cardboard scratcher.

Where should I put a cat scratching post?

Place it in a busy area where your cat spends time, such as next to the sofa, near a window or by the spot where your cat sleeps. Cats like to scratch after waking up, so a post near the bed or cat tree works well.

How many scratching posts does a cat need?

A common rule is at least one scratcher per cat, plus one more, placed in different rooms. Mixing vertical posts with a horizontal or angled scratcher gives your cat choices.
