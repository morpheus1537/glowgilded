How to choose a cat tree for large cats

  1. Base size and weight: A wide, heavy base is the best defense against wobbling.
  2. Perch and condo size: Measure your cat stretched out; large cats need perches around 20 inches wide.
  3. Anti-tip hardware: A wall strap or anti-tip kit is a must for tall trees.
  4. Sisal posts: More, thicker posts give more scratching surface.
  5. Height: Tall trees are great for lookouts; make sure the ceiling and room allow it.
  6. Cleaning: Removable or washable parts make hair and dander easier to manage.

Large cat tree tips

  • Anchor it: Always use the anti-tip kit, and place tall trees in a corner or against a wall.
  • Pick a window: Cats love a view; a tree by a window gets the most use.
  • Check bolts: Re-tighten screws every few months as cats climb.
  • Vacuum often: Plush collects fur; a quick vacuum keeps it fresh. For the rest of the house, see how to get pet hair off the couch.
  • Protect floors from litter, too: A cat litter mat stops tracking near the box.

Cat tree vs. wall shelves

Wall-mounted shelves save floor space and look sleek, but they need drilling into studs and a careful layout for heavy cats. A freestanding cat tree combines climbing, scratching, napping and hiding in one piece, needs no drilling and can move with you. For most renters and large cats, a sturdy tree is the easier choice.

Frequently asked questions

What size cat tree do I need for a large cat?

Look for wide perches and condos, a heavy, wide base and thick posts. As a guide, the Yaheetech here has a 21 by 15 inch top perch and a 30.5 by 19 inch base, and Globlazer's S72 has a 21.7 by 11.8 inch platform.

How do I keep a cat tree from tipping over?

Choose a wide base, place the tree in a corner or against a wall, and use the anti-tip kit. Yaheetech, Gitelsnour and Hey-brother all include an anti-tip kit or device.

How much weight can a cat tree hold?

It depends on the part. Yaheetech lists its condo for cats up to 22 lb and its hammock up to 18 lb. Check the limits for each perch, condo and hammock before buying.

Is a tall cat tree better for big cats?

Cats love height for watching their territory, but stability matters more than height for heavy cats. A tall tree with a wide base and an anti-tip kit is the best of both.

How do I get my cat to use a new cat tree?

Put it near a window or where your cat already likes to sit, rub catnip on the sisal, and use treats or a wand toy to encourage climbing.
