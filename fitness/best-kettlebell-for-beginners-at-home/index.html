How to choose your first kettlebell

  1. Weight: Yes4All suggests 5 to 35 pounds for beginners. Lighter bells suit presses; heavier bells suit two-handed swings and deadlifts.
  2. Handle: A wide, smooth handle with room for two hands is easiest for swings and goblet squats.
  3. Coating: Vinyl and neoprene protect floors and reduce noise. Powder-coated cast iron grips well without chalk.
  4. Flat base: A flat bottom keeps the bell upright for storage and floor moves like renegade rows.
  5. One-piece build: Solid cast iron without welds is less likely to loosen over time.
  6. Fixed vs. adjustable: Adjustable bells save space as you progress; fixed bells are simpler and more compact.

Beginner kettlebell tips

  • Learn the hinge first: The kettlebell swing is a hip hinge, not a squat. Practice the movement without weight.
  • Start with three moves: Goblet squats, deadlifts and two-handed swings build a strong base.
  • Use a mat: A thick mat protects floors and knees. See our best thick yoga mats for bad knees.
  • Clear the space: Give yourself room in front and behind for swings.
  • Progress slowly: Add reps before adding weight.

Frequently asked questions

What weight kettlebell should a beginner start with?

Yes4All suggests 5 to 35 pounds for beginners, 40 to 65 pounds for intermediate users and 70 pounds and up for advanced training. Many beginners pick a lighter bell for presses and a heavier one for two-handed swings and deadlifts. If you are unsure, ask a trainer or start lighter.

Is one kettlebell enough for a home workout?

One kettlebell covers a lot of full-body moves, including swings, goblet squats, deadlifts, rows and presses. As you get stronger you will likely want a second weight, or an adjustable bell like the BowFlex SelectTech 840 that dials from 8 to 40 pounds.

Are vinyl or cast iron kettlebells better?

Both have a cast iron core in the Yes4All picks. Vinyl and neoprene coatings reduce noise and protect floors, while bare or powder-coated cast iron gives a firm, chalk-free grip that many people prefer for swings.

Can I use a kettlebell in an apartment?

Yes, with care. Choose a vinyl or neoprene-coated bell, train on a thick mat and avoid dropping it. Amazon Basics also recommends using its powder-coated bell on rubber or padded surfaces.

Are adjustable kettlebells good for beginners?

They can be. An adjustable kettlebell replaces several fixed bells, which saves space and money as you progress. The trade-off is a bulkier shape than a classic one-piece bell.
