A simple 20-minute routine with this gear

  • Warm up (3 minutes): march in place, arm circles and gentle hip openers on the mat.
  • Strength circuit (12 minutes): banded squats, glute bridges, lateral walks and push-ups. Do 40 seconds on, 20 seconds rest, three rounds.
  • Core (3 minutes): planks, dead bugs and bicycle crunches on the mat.
  • Recovery (2 minutes plus): roll calves, quads and upper back on the foam roller.

Tips for sticking with home workouts

  • Keep gear visible: a rolled mat by the couch is a daily reminder.
  • Schedule it: treat a workout like a meeting on your calendar.
  • Progress slowly: move to the next band level only when the current one feels easy for full sets.
  • Track trends, not days: weigh in at the same time each morning and look at weekly averages.

Frequently asked questions

What equipment do I need to start working out at home?

A comfortable mat and a set of resistance bands are enough for a full-body routine. A foam roller helps with recovery, and a scale or tape measure helps you track progress.

Are resistance bands as good as weights?

For beginners and general fitness, bands can build real strength, especially in the glutes, hips and shoulders. They are lighter and safer for small spaces, though heavy lifters will eventually want weights too.

How often should I foam roll?

Many people roll for five to ten minutes after workouts or on rest days. Move slowly, avoid rolling directly on joints or the lower back, and stop if anything feels sharp rather than tight.

How accurate are smart body fat scales?

Body fat numbers from any bathroom scale are estimates, so focus on the trend over weeks instead of a single reading. Weigh yourself at the same time of day for the most consistent results.
