How to choose a foam roller for your back

  1. Firmness: Soft or medium is best to start; firm and bumpy rollers press deeper.
  2. Texture: Smooth spreads pressure; ridges and knobs target knots.
  3. Length: 36 inches covers the whole back and works for lengthwise stretches; 12 to 18 inches is compact.
  4. Shape: Round rollers roll; half-round rollers stay put for gentle stretches and balance work.
  5. Core: Solid or strong cores keep their shape longer than hollow, soft foam.

Foam rolling tips for a tight back

  • Go slow: Roll about an inch per second and pause on tight spots instead of rushing.
  • Support your head: Clasp your hands behind your head when rolling your upper back.
  • Ease off the lower spine: Roll the glutes, hips and sides instead of pressing hard on the low back.
  • Breathe: Relax into the roller; tensing up works against you.
  • Fix the cause too: A better desk setup helps; see our ergonomic home office accessories for back pain.
  • Stop if it hurts: Discomfort is normal, sharp pain is not.

Frequently asked questions

Can you foam roll your lower back?

Be gentle. Many trainers suggest rolling the muscles around the lower back, like the glutes and hips, rather than pressing hard on the lower spine. 321 STRONG says its medium-density roller is soft enough not to hurt the spine when rolled directly. If you have an injury or ongoing pain, ask a doctor or physical therapist first.

Should a beginner use a soft or firm foam roller?

Start softer. A smooth or softer roller like the TriggerPoint CORE or a medium-density textured roller is easier to tolerate. Firm, bumpy rollers like the RumbleRoller suit people with foam rolling experience.

What size foam roller is best for your back?

A 36-inch roller like the Amazon Basics spans your whole back and is stable when you lie on it lengthwise. Shorter 12- to 18-inch rollers are easier to store and travel with and work well for targeted areas.

How long should you foam roll?

Many people roll each area for 30 to 60 seconds, moving slowly and breathing. 321 STRONG suggests rolling for about 20 minutes after exercise and again over the following two days.

Is a textured foam roller better than a smooth one?

Not better, just different. Textured rollers press more into knots, while smooth rollers spread pressure more evenly and feel gentler. Many people start smooth and add textured later.
