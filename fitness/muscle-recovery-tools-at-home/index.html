Tips for better recovery at home

  • Start gentle: with massage guns and lacrosse balls, use the lowest speed or lightest pressure first and build up.
  • Keep it short: a minute or two per muscle group is usually plenty for a massage gun. Do not stay on one spot for long.
  • Avoid sensitive areas: skip the front of the neck, bones, joints and any injured, bruised or swollen area.
  • Add heat at the end of the day: a heated massager or warm bath is a nice way to relax tight muscles before bed.
  • Hydrate and sleep: no tool replaces good sleep, water and rest days.
  • Ask a pro when needed: sharp, lasting or injury-related pain deserves a doctor or physical therapist.

What to look for in recovery tools

  • Adjustability: multiple speeds or intensity levels let you match the tool to how sore you are.
  • Noise: if you will use it in front of the TV or at the office, quieter is better.
  • Power: check whether a tool is cordless, USB-charged or plug-in before you buy.
  • Portability: small tools like lacrosse balls are easy to bring on trips.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best tools for muscle recovery at home?

A good home kit covers a few different needs: a massage gun for larger muscles, a firm massage ball for targeted knots, a heated massager for the neck and shoulders, and a warm bath soak to wind down. Add a foam roller if you already train regularly.

Is a massage gun better than a foam roller?

They do different jobs. A foam roller covers large areas using your body weight, while a massage gun lets you control speed and target specific spots without getting on the floor. Many people use both.

How do you use a lacrosse ball for muscle knots?

Place the ball between the sore spot and a wall or the floor, then lean into it and use your body weight to apply pressure. Roll slowly, pause on tender spots and ease off if anything feels sharp rather than just tight.

How long should I soak in an Epsom salt bath?

Dr Teal's recommends soaking for 20 minutes. Use warm, not scalding, water, and follow the directions on the package for how much salt to add.

When should I see a professional instead of using recovery tools?

Home tools are for everyday tightness and soreness. If you have sharp pain, pain from an injury, numbness, swelling or pain that does not improve after a few days, talk to a doctor or physical therapist before using a massager on the area.
