How to choose adjustable dumbbells

  • Weight range: think about your heaviest lift, often rows or goblet squats. If you are new, a set reaching 25 to 50 lb per hand leaves room to grow.
  • Increment size: smaller jumps (2.5 lb) make it easier to progress on shoulder and arm exercises.
  • Adjustment style: selector dumbbells (PowerBlock, NordicTrack) change weight in seconds. Plate-and-collar sets (FEIERDUN, Amazon Basics) are slower but flexible.
  • Footprint: measure where the set will live. Trays or a case help keep the floor clear.
  • Future-proofing: expansion kits like PowerBlock's let you add weight later instead of buying a new set.
  • Protect your floor: put a rubber mat under your training area.

A simple starter routine

Two or three times a week, try three rounds of goblet squats, dumbbell rows, floor presses, Romanian deadlifts and shoulder presses, 8 to 12 reps each. Pick a weight where the last two reps feel hard but your form stays clean, and add weight in small steps over time. If you have an injury or health condition, check with a professional before starting a new program.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best adjustable dumbbells for a home gym?

For most people building a home gym, the PowerBlock Elite EXP 90 Stage 1 is a strong choice because it adjusts from 5 to 50 lb per hand, takes up little space and can be expanded later. If you want quick selector-style changes, the NordicTrack 55 lb Select-a-Weight pair is a good alternative.

Are adjustable dumbbells worth it?

Yes, if you are short on space. One adjustable pair can replace many fixed dumbbells; PowerBlock says its set replaces 16 pairs and NordicTrack says its pair offers the value of 30 individual dumbbells.

What weight adjustable dumbbells should a beginner buy?

Beginners often do well with a set that starts light and goes up to around 25 to 50 lb per hand. Lighter plate-style sets like the Amazon Basics 38 lb set are good for toning, while a 5 to 50 lb set gives you room to progress for years.

Are spin-lock or selector dumbbells better?

Selector dumbbells, like PowerBlock and NordicTrack, change weight in seconds, which helps during circuits. Spin-lock or threaded sets, like FEIERDUN and Amazon Basics, take longer to change but are usually more affordable and flexible.

Do I need a mat under adjustable dumbbells?

It is a good idea. A rubber mat protects your floor, reduces noise and gives the dumbbells a stable place to rest between sets.
