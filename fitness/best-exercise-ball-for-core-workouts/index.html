How to choose an exercise ball for core workouts

  1. Size by height: Most brands size balls in centimeters (45, 55, 65, 75, 85 cm). Use the brand's chart. Your hips should be level with or slightly above your knees when sitting.
  2. Anti-burst design: Look for slow-deflation or burst-resistant material so a puncture does not drop you to the floor.
  3. Weight capacity: Check the tested or listed weight limit, especially if you plan to use weights on the ball.
  4. Grip: A textured, anti-slip surface keeps the ball steady on hard floors and under sweaty hands.
  5. Pump included: Every pick here includes a pump, which saves hunting for one.
  6. Ball or dome: A full ball is best for floor core work and sitting. A half-ball dome is better for standing balance drills.

Core exercises to try on a stability ball

  • Ball crunch: Lie with the ball under your lower back, feet flat, and curl up slowly.
  • Plank on the ball: Rest your forearms on the ball and hold a straight line from head to heels.
  • Stir the pot: From a forearm plank on the ball, make small circles with your elbows.
  • Ball pass: Lying on your back, pass the ball from your hands to your feet and back.
  • Hamstring curl: Heels on the ball, lift your hips and roll the ball toward you.
  • Dead bug hold: Squeeze the ball between your hands and knees while extending the opposite arm and leg.

Safety tips

  • Inflate to the right size: An underinflated ball is wobbly; an overinflated one is harder to control.
  • Clear the area: Keep sharp objects, furniture corners and pets' claws away.
  • Use a mat: A thick yoga mat adds grip and cushions knees during kneeling moves.
  • Start slow: Master each move with control before adding speed or weights.
  • Check for wear: Inspect the ball regularly and replace it if you see cracks or thinning.

Frequently asked questions

What size exercise ball should I get?

Match the ball to your height. As a general guide from these listings, a 55 cm ball suits people about 5'0" to 5'5" and a 65 cm ball suits about 5'7" to 6'1"; check each brand's size chart before ordering.

Are exercise balls good for core workouts?

Yes. Because the ball is unstable, moves like crunches, planks, pikes and bridges on a ball make your core work to keep you balanced, and makers like BOSU say this engages more muscles.

What does anti-burst mean on an exercise ball?

An anti-burst ball is designed to deflate slowly if punctured instead of popping suddenly. Trideer and APEXUP use a honeycomb structure for this, and THERABAND and BOSU describe their material as burst-resistant.

Can I use an exercise ball as a desk chair?

You can for short periods. Trideer, APEXUP, THERABAND and Gaiam all suggest active sitting, but alternate with a regular chair and choose the right size so your knees are about level with your hips.

Why is my new exercise ball smaller than the listed size?

New balls need time to stretch. Trideer suggests letting the ball sit 2-3 days after first inflation and then topping it up, and Gaiam suggests inflating, waiting 24 hours, deflating halfway and filling again.
