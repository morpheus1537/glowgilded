How to choose a massage gun for sore muscles

  1. Stall force: How much pressure the gun can take before it stalls. Higher numbers suit deep tissue work on big muscles.
  2. Amplitude: Stroke depth. 10 to 12mm feels deep, while shorter strokes feel gentler.
  3. Speeds: More speed levels let you start gently and build up.
  4. Attachments: Ball heads suit large muscles, flat heads suit dense areas and bullet or thumb heads target trigger points.
  5. Noise: Quieter guns, around 40 to 50 dB, are easier to use while watching TV or at the office.
  6. Weight and grip: Lighter guns and angled grips make it easier to reach your own back.

How to use a massage gun safely

  • Start low: Begin on the lowest speed with a soft or ball head, then increase if it feels good.
  • Keep it moving: Glide slowly along the muscle instead of holding it in one spot.
  • Limit time: About 30 seconds to 2 minutes per muscle group is plenty.
  • Let the gun do the work: Do not press hard; light pressure is usually enough.
  • Avoid sensitive areas: Skip bones, joints, the spine, the front of the neck and any injuries.
  • Use it before and after: A quick pass can help you warm up, and a longer one can help you wind down after training.

Massage gun vs foam roller

A foam roller uses your body weight to apply broad pressure and is great for the back, hips and IT band, but it can be hard to get on the floor and target small areas. A massage gun is more precise and easier on the knees and wrists, and it reaches calves, forearms and shoulders well. Many people use both. Frequently asked questions

Do massage guns help with sore muscles?

Many people use massage guns after workouts to ease tightness and feel looser. They are a recovery aid, not a medical treatment, so see a doctor for persistent pain or injuries.

How long should you use a massage gun on one muscle?

Short sessions work best. Spend about 30 seconds to 2 minutes per muscle group, keep the gun moving, and stop if anything feels sharp or painful. Several picks here auto-shut off after 10 minutes.

Where should you not use a massage gun?

Avoid the front and sides of the neck, the spine, joints, bones, bruises and any injured or swollen areas. Stick to large, fleshy muscles like the calves, quads, glutes and upper back.

Is a stronger massage gun always better?

No. Higher stall force and amplitude reach deeper, but they can be too intense for sensitive users. A gentler model like Theragun Relief suits daily comfort, while deep tissue guns suit athletes and larger muscles.

What does amplitude mean on a massage gun?

Amplitude is how far the head travels with each stroke. TOLOCO and Mebak list 12mm and RENPHO lists 10mm; a longer stroke generally feels deeper.
