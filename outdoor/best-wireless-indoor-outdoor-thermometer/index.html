What to look for in a wireless thermometer

  1. Range: How far the outdoor sensor can be from the display. Longer range helps with detached garages or sheds.
  2. Update interval: Most sensors send new readings every 30 seconds or so.
  3. High and low records: Useful for checking how cold it got overnight.
  4. Humidity: A hygrometer helps you manage dry winter air indoors.
  5. Display and power: A large, backlit or USB-powered display is easier to read in the dark.
  6. Extra sensors: Multi-sensor kits can watch the nursery, basement and garage at once.

Setup tips for accurate readings

  • Shade the sensor: Direct sun can make an outdoor sensor read many degrees too high.
  • Keep it dry: Mount it under an eave or in a small shelter, even if it is weather-resistant.
  • Avoid heat sources: Stay away from dryer vents, grills, AC units and dark walls.
  • Test the range first: Set up the display and sensor in their final spots before mounting anything.
  • Swap batteries before deep winter: Cold weather drains batteries faster.
  • Plan for snow days: Pair your thermometer with the right snow removal tools.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I put the outdoor sensor of a wireless thermometer?

Place it in the shade, out of direct sun and rain, and away from walls that radiate heat, such as near a dryer vent. A spot under an eave on the north side of the house usually gives the most accurate readings.

How far can a wireless outdoor thermometer sensor be from the display?

It depends on the model. Both AcuRite thermometers here list up to 165 feet, the Unni lists up to 330 feet and the ThermoPro TempAir lists up to 500 feet. Walls and metal siding reduce real-world range.

Do wireless indoor outdoor thermometers measure humidity?

Some do. The Unni and ThermoPro models measure temperature and humidity, the La Crosse shows indoor humidity, and the two AcuRite models here measure temperature only.

Why does my outdoor thermometer read too high?

The most common cause is direct sunlight on the sensor. Move it to a shaded, ventilated spot and give it a few minutes to settle.

Can I use an outdoor thermometer sensor in a garage or freezer?

Many people use them to monitor garages, sheds, greenhouses and RVs. ThermoPro specifically mentions detached garages, greenhouses and RVs. Check the listed temperature range before putting a sensor somewhere very cold.
