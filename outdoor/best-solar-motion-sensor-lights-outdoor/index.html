How to choose solar motion lights

  1. Brightness: Up to 2,500 lumens suits driveways and yards. Smaller lights suit steps and doors.
  2. Coverage: Wide-angle or multi-head lights cover more area from one spot.
  3. Sensor range: About 26 feet is typical; some reach 30 feet.
  4. Modes: Look for motion-only, dim-with-motion-boost and always-on options.
  5. Weatherproofing: IP65 is the common rating for rain and snow.
  6. Pack size: Two big lights or six small ones? Map out your spots before you buy.
  7. Sun exposure: Every solar light depends on its panel getting direct sun.

Installation tips

  • Find the sun: Mount where the panel gets the most direct sun, especially in winter.
  • Mind the height: About 6.5 to 8 feet works best for most motion sensors.
  • Avoid other lights: Porch lights and street lights can confuse sensors.
  • Charge first: Give new lights a full day of sun before judging them.
  • Clean the panel: Brush off snow, dust and leaves regularly. Our snow removal tools guide can help after storms.

Frequently asked questions

Do solar motion sensor lights work in winter?

They can, but short days and low sun mean less charging. Mount the panel where it gets the most direct sun, keep it clear of snow and dust, and expect shorter run times on cloudy stretches. Tuffenough suggests switching its lights off for 2 to 3 days to fully recharge in poor weather.

How far do solar motion sensors detect?

Most lights here list a PIR sensor range of about 26 feet. The MIHANI lists up to 30 feet with a 180-degree detection range.

How high should I mount a solar motion light?

Tuffenough and MIHANI recommend about 6.5 to 8 feet, with the solar panel facing direct sunlight. Mounting too high or too low can affect how well the motion sensor works.

Are solar motion lights bright enough for security?

Larger flood-style lights like the Aootek 2500 lumen, Tuffenough and MIHANI are listed at up to 2,500 lumens, bright enough for driveways and yards. Smaller step and fence lights are better for paths and doors.

Do solar lights need batteries?

They have built-in rechargeable batteries charged by the panel, so you do not need to buy batteries or run wires. Aootek lists a 2,200mAh battery on its 2,500 lumen light and Tuffenough lists 1,800mAh.
