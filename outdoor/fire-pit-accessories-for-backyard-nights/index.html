Fire pit safety and buying tips

  • Start the fire the right way. Use a natural starter like fatwood and dry, seasoned wood. Never use gasoline to start or revive a fire.
  • Keep tools within reach. Gloves and tongs should live next to the fire pit, not in the garage.
  • Mind the distance. Place the fire pit on a non-flammable surface, well away from the house, fences, overhanging branches and patio furniture, and follow your fire pit manufacturer's clearance guidance.
  • Have a way to put it out. Keep a bucket of water, a hose or a fire extinguisher nearby.
  • Deal with ashes carefully. Ashes can hold hot embers for days. Store them in a lidded metal container away from anything that burns.
  • Check local rules. Some towns restrict open burning or require permits, especially on dry or windy days.

Frequently asked questions

What accessories do you need for a fire pit?

The core set is a way to light the fire (fatwood or another fire starter), tools to move logs (tongs and heat-resistant gloves), and a metal ash bucket with a lid for cleanup. Roasting sticks are the fun extra that gets everyone outside.

What is fatwood and is it safe to use in a fire pit?

Fatwood is resin-rich pine that lights quickly. Billy Buckskin says its sticks are 100% natural with no added chemicals and are made for fireplaces, fire pits, wood stoves and campfires; the brand suggests 2 to 3 sticks among your kindling.

How hot can fire pit gloves handle?

The RAPICCA gloves are rated heat resistant up to 932°F (500°C) according to the listing. Even so, use them for brief contact with hot logs and tools, not for holding burning wood for long periods.

How long should ashes cool before you throw them away?

Ashes can hold live embers for a long time, so let them cool fully, store them in a metal container with a tight lid away from the house and anything flammable, and check local guidance before disposal.

Are long roasting sticks safe for kids?

Long sticks help. The MalloMe sticks keep hands 32 inches from the fire and have colored tips so each child knows which stick is theirs, but children should always be supervised around a fire pit.
