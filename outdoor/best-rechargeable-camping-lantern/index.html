How to choose a rechargeable camping lantern

  1. Brightness: 1,000 lumens lights a campsite. Lower-lumen lanterns are enough inside a tent.
  2. Runtime: Check how long the lantern lasts on a charge, and at what brightness.
  3. Light colors: Warm light is cozier and less harsh at night. Daylight white is better for tasks.
  4. Water resistance: IPX4 or IP44 handles splashes and rain. IP67 and IP68 survive submersion.
  5. Power bank: Some lanterns can charge a phone in an emergency.
  6. Size and weight: Backpackers need a light, packable lantern. Car campers can choose bigger, brighter ones.
  7. Hanging options: Hooks or handles let you hang the lantern from a tent loop or branch.

Camping lantern tips

  • Charge before every trip: Top up the battery the night before you leave.
  • Use warm light at camp: Warm modes are easier on the eyes and attract fewer bugs than bright white.
  • Hang it high: Hanging the lantern from a tent loop or branch spreads light more evenly.
  • Dim to save battery: Lower brightness can stretch runtime a lot.
  • Keep one in the house: A charged lantern is a great backup for winter outages.

Frequently asked questions

How many lumens do you need in a camping lantern?

For lighting a tent or picnic table, 300 to 1,000 lumens is plenty, and most lanterns let you dim down to save battery. Brighter lanterns like the 1,800-lumen AYL are useful for lighting a whole campsite or a room during a power outage.

How long does a rechargeable camping lantern last on one charge?

It depends on brightness. Lepro, AYL and AlpsWolf rate their lanterns for up to 12 hours, LuminAID lists up to 24 hours, and Glocusent says its lantern lasts up to 80 hours on the lowest setting.

Can a camping lantern charge my phone?

Some can. The Lepro and AYL lanterns work as power banks, and AlpsWolf says you can charge a phone in an emergency. The LuminAID and Glocusent listings do not mention phone charging.

Are rechargeable lanterns good for power outages?

Yes. Rechargeable LED lanterns are safer indoors than gas lanterns and candles, and several picks here are marketed for outages and hurricanes. Keep them charged and store them where you can find them in the dark.

Is a solar camping lantern worth it?

A solar lantern like the LuminAID Nova is handy for long trips and emergency kits because it can recharge in direct sunlight, though LuminAID says a full solar charge takes about 10 hours. It also charges by USB in 1 to 2 hours.
