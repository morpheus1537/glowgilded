How to choose an outdoor doormat for winter

  1. Material: All-rubber mats are the toughest and rinse clean. Fabric-topped mats with rubber backing trap more fine dirt and water.
  2. Scraping surface: Raised ridges, lattices or bristle-like textures pull snow and mud off treads.
  3. Water control: A raised border or water dam stops meltwater from spreading.
  4. Weight and backing: Heavier mats and rubber backings resist sliding and blowing away.
  5. Size: A 2 x 3 foot mat covers most front doors. Bigger is better for double doors or busy entries.
  6. Thickness: Measure door clearance if your door swings outward over the mat.

Tips for a cleaner winter entry

  • Use two mats: Put a rubber scraper outside and an absorbent mat just inside to catch what is left.
  • Shovel the landing: Clear and treat the steps so less snow reaches the mat.
  • Hose it off on mild days: Rinsing salt and grit keeps mats working and protects your porch.
  • Add a boot tray: A tray inside the door gives wet boots somewhere to drip.
  • Check for ice: Meltwater can refreeze around a mat, so check steps on cold mornings.
  • Keep a brush nearby: A stiff brush helps knock packed snow off treads.

Rubber vs fabric-topped mats

All-rubber mats like the A1 HOME COLLECTIONS, Calloway Mills and Ottomanson are nearly indestructible outdoors. They scrape well, drain or shed water and clean with a hose, but they do not absorb moisture. Fabric-topped mats with rubber backings, like WaterHog and Gorilla Grip, catch finer dirt and hold water in their surface and borders, which keeps more of it off the floor. For snowy climates, a rubber mat outside and a water-trapping mat inside is the best combination. Frequently asked questions

What type of doormat is best for snow?

A rubber-backed mat with raised ridges or a water-trapping border works best. The ridges scrape snow and slush off boots, and a raised border, like WaterHog's, holds the meltwater instead of letting it run onto the porch or floor.

Should outdoor doormats be rubber or coir?

Rubber handles snow, ice melt and standing water better and rinses clean with a hose. Coir looks traditional and scrapes well but can stay soggy and break down faster in wet winter weather.

How do I keep my outdoor doormat from sliding?

Choose a heavy mat or one with a rubber backing. The A1 HOME COLLECTIONS mat weighs 9 pounds so it stays put, and rubber backings on the other picks also help grip the porch.

How do you clean a muddy outdoor doormat?

Shake or sweep off loose debris, spray it with a garden hose and let it air dry. Every mat in this guide is listed as hose clean or easy to rinse.

Will a thick doormat block my door?

It can. Measure the gap under your door if it opens over the mat. The Gorilla Grip WeatherMAX is .3 inch thick, the WaterHog is 3/8 inch, and the Calloway Mills Gatsby is 0.5 inch.
