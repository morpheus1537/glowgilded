How to choose a snow sled for kids

  1. Sled type: Toboggans are stable and can carry two riders. Saucers spin and go fast but are harder to steer. Foam sleds are softer on bumps.
  2. Age and weight: Check the age and weight limits. Flybar lists its sleds for ages 5 and up and up to 110 lbs.
  3. Durability in the cold: Double-walled or cold-resistant plastic is less likely to crack in freezing temps.
  4. Handles: Sturdy handles help kids hold on and control the ride.
  5. Pull rope: A pull rope makes the walk back uphill easier and lets you tow little ones on flat ground.
  6. Storage: Big toboggans take up garage space, while saucers are compact.

Sledding safety tips for kids

  • Pick the right hill: Choose a gentle slope with a long, flat area at the bottom, away from roads, trees, fences and water.
  • Feet first: Have kids ride sitting up and feet first, not head first.
  • Wear a helmet: A ski or bike helmet adds protection on faster runs.
  • One at a time: Wait until the path is clear before the next rider goes.
  • Dress in layers: Waterproof gloves, boots and snow pants keep kids out longer. Our cold weather running gear guide has ideas for warm layers for grown-ups.
  • Never tow with a vehicle: Pull sleds by hand only.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best type of sled for kids?

For most kids, a toboggan-style sled with handles is easiest to control and can fit a parent too. Foam sleds give a softer, cushioned ride, and saucers are fun and fast but harder to steer.

What size sled should I get for my child?

Match the sled to the rider. Flybar's 36-inch toboggan and 26-inch saucer are for ages 5 and up and hold up to 110 lbs, while the 4-foot GoSports sled fits 2 riders so a parent can ride with a child.

Are foam or plastic sleds better?

Foam sleds like Flybar's absorb bumps for a smoother ride and are lightweight. Hard plastic sleds like the Retrospec and GoSports are typically faster on packed snow, and double-walled plastic resists cracking.

How do I keep kids safe while sledding?

Choose a gentle hill with a long, flat runout away from roads, trees and ponds, have kids sit feet-first, and consider a helmet. Never tow a sled with a vehicle; GoSports specifically warns against towing with any motor vehicle.

Will a plastic sled crack in the cold?

Cheap single-layer sleds can. GoSports uses a thick double-walled HDPE design that it says resists cracking, and Retrospec says its cold-resistant coating handles temperatures down to -22°F.
