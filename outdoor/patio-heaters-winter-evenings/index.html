Patio heater safety tips

  • Read the manual first: Clearance, ventilation and setup rules vary by model.
  • Keep clearance: Keep heaters away from umbrellas, awnings, plants, curtains and anything flammable.
  • Use on a stable surface: Place towers on flat, level ground and use the weighted base or water box.
  • Check for leaks: Before each season, check propane connections with soapy water and follow the manufacturer's leak-check instructions.
  • Never leave it unattended: Turn the heater and tank off when you go inside.
  • Store tanks outdoors: Keep propane cylinders outside and upright, away from heat.

Buying tips

  • Match BTU to space: Small radiant heaters warm a few chairs; 40,000 BTU and up suit decks and dining areas.
  • Plan your fuel: Decide between 1 lb tanks and 20 lb cylinders, and check what accessories you need.
  • Look for safety features: Tip-over shut-off is a must for any heater people walk around.
  • Think about storage: Tower heaters are tall; consider where it will live off-season.
  • Check local rules: Some apartments and HOAs restrict propane on balconies.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best patio heater for a small patio?

For a small patio or balcony-sized seating area where propane is allowed, a portable radiant heater like the Mr. Heater Buddy is a good fit. It lists 4,000 or 9,000 BTU settings and heats spaces up to 225 sq. ft. A tall tower heater is better for larger decks.

How long does a 20 lb propane tank last in a patio heater?

It depends on the heater's BTU output and setting. Mr. Heater lists up to 108 hours for the Buddy on a 20 lb cylinder with an optional hose and filter, while a 46,000 BTU tower heater on high will use propane much faster. Check the manufacturer's guidance for your model.

Can I use a propane patio heater on a covered porch?

Follow the manufacturer's clearance and ventilation rules for your specific heater and check local codes. Tall patio heaters need open-air use with plenty of overhead clearance, and many are not meant for enclosed or low-ceiling spaces.

Are tabletop or tower patio heaters better?

Tower heaters spread heat over a wider area and suit decks and dining sets. Smaller portable heaters warm a couple of chairs and are easier to store and move. Choose based on how many people you want to keep warm.

What safety features should a patio heater have?

Look for a tip-over shut-off at minimum. The Amazon Basics and Pamapic towers list tip-over shut-off valves, the Pamapic adds flame-failure protection and an overheat guard, and the Mr. Heater Little Buddy lists both tip-over and low-oxygen shut-off.
