How to choose a patio pressure washer

  1. Rated PSI and GPM: Working pressure and water flow matter more than peak numbers.
  2. Nozzles: A set of quick-connect tips (0°, 15°, 25°, 40° and soap) covers most jobs.
  3. Hose length: A longer high-pressure hose means moving the machine less.
  4. Soap system: Onboard tanks or a foam cannon make it easier to apply cleaners.
  5. Storage: Compact units with wheels or handles are easier to tuck away.

Patio pressure washing tips

  • Sweep first: Clear leaves and loose debris before washing.
  • Start wide: Use a 25° or 40° nozzle and only go narrower for stubborn spots.
  • Work in sections: Keep the wand moving in overlapping passes to avoid stripes.
  • Protect plants: Rinse nearby plants with plain water before and after using cleaners.
  • Winterize: Drain hoses and the pump before cold weather; our snow removal tools guide covers the season ahead.

Frequently asked questions

What PSI do I need to clean a patio?

For most concrete and stone patios, a homeowner electric pressure washer rated around 1,800 to 2,000 working PSI is plenty. The picks here list rated pressures from 1,800 to 2,030 PSI.

Can a pressure washer damage pavers or a wood deck?

Yes, if you use too narrow a nozzle or hold it too close. Start with a wide 25° or 40° nozzle, keep the tip moving and test a hidden spot first, especially on wood, sealed pavers and joint sand.

What is the difference between max PSI and rated PSI?

Max PSI is a peak figure, while rated PSI is closer to steady working pressure. For example, Westinghouse lists the ePX3500 at 2,500 max PSI and 2,000 rated PSI.

Do I need a surface cleaner attachment?

It is optional. A rotating surface cleaner can speed up large flat areas and reduce striping, but a wide nozzle works for smaller patios. Make sure any attachment fits your washer's connector and pressure rating.

Should I store a pressure washer outside in winter?

No. Water left in the pump can freeze and cause damage, so drain the unit and store it somewhere above freezing, following the manufacturer's winterizing steps.
