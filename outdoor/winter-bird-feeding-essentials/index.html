Winter bird feeding tips

  • Feed consistently. Birds learn where food is; keep feeders stocked through cold snaps.
  • Offer seed, suet and water. This mix attracts the most species.
  • Clear snow. Brush snow off feeder ports and the bath after storms.
  • Clean regularly. Wash feeders and baths to keep birds healthy.
  • Give birds cover. Place feeders near shrubs or trees so birds can escape predators.

How to set up a winter bird station

Hang the Squirrel Buster and suet feeder where you can see them from a favorite window, mount the heated bath on a nearby railing or stand, and add a cozy spot for yourself to watch. Frequently asked questions

Should you feed birds in winter?

Many backyard birders feed most heavily in winter, when natural food is scarce and birds need extra energy to stay warm. A mix of seed, suet and fresh water attracts the widest variety of birds.

What is the best food for birds in winter?

Sunflower seed and suet are two of the most popular winter foods. A blend like Wagner's Deluxe, which contains sunflower and general-purpose seeds, attracts both perching and ground birds, while suet cakes draw woodpeckers, nuthatches and chickadees.

Do birds need water in winter?

Yes. When ponds and puddles freeze, open water can be hard to find. A heated bird bath like the API 600 uses a built-in thermostat to keep water from freezing.

How do you keep squirrels off a bird feeder?

A weight-activated feeder like the Brome Squirrel Buster closes its feeding ports when a squirrel climbs on, so birds can feed and squirrels cannot. Brome says squirrels are foiled but not harmed.

Where should I put a bird feeder in winter?

Place feeders where you can see them from a window, near shrubs or trees that give birds cover, and away from spots where squirrels can jump onto them. Keep feeders and baths clean and stocked through the season.
