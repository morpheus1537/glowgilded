How to choose toddler stocking stuffers

  • Check age labels: some toys are 18 months and up, while crayons and Play-Doh are 3 and up.
  • Avoid tiny parts: skip anything small enough to be a choking hazard for younger toddlers.
  • Think about mess: water-reveal pads and twistable crayons are easy on Christmas morning.
  • Mix it up: combine one creative, one bath and one sensory gift for variety.

Frequently asked questions

What do you put in a toddler's stocking?

Small things they can use right away: twist-up crayons, a water-reveal coloring pad, a bath toy, play dough and sensory toys. Skip tiny pieces and check the age label on every item.

Are Crayola Twistables good for toddlers?

Yes. They twist up instead of needing sharpening or peeling, and the mini size fits little hands; the set is labeled for ages 3 and up, so supervise younger toddlers.

What age is Play-Doh for?

The Play-Doh Starter Set is labeled for ages 3 and up. The compound is made mostly of flour, salt and water and contains wheat, so skip it for children with a wheat allergy.

Is Aqua Art really mess-free?

Yes. Kids fill the brush pen with water and color to reveal hidden colors; the pages fade as they dry and can be reused, so there are no paints or markers involved.

What is the best bath toy for an 18-month-old?

The Nuby Floating Octopus is a great choice. It is designed for kids 18 months and up, floats, and comes with three rings to toss and stack, which builds hand-eye coordination.

The bottom line

For most toddlers, Crayola Mini Twistables are the best stocking stuffer, because they are easy to use, last a long time and are less messy than classic crayons. Add Play-Doh for creative play, the Nuby octopus for bath time, Bluey Aqua Art for travel, and pop tubes for sensory fun.
