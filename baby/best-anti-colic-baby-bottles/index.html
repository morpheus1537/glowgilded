How to choose an anti-colic bottle

  1. Vent design: Internal vents, base vents, valves in the nipple and multi-vent nipples all aim to keep air out of your baby's tummy.
  2. Number of parts: More parts can mean more effective venting but more cleaning.
  3. Nipple shape and flow: Breast-like shapes help babies who switch between breast and bottle. Start newborns on a slow flow.
  4. Material: Plastic is light and durable; silicone feels soft and squeezable.
  5. Sterilizing: Self-sterilizing bottles save time if you do not own a sterilizer.
  6. Buy a few first: Babies can be picky, so try a small pack before stocking up.

Tips for gas-free feeds

  • Feed upright: Keep your baby's head higher than their tummy.
  • Tilt the bottle: Keep milk filling the nipple so your baby does not gulp air.
  • Pace the feed: Let your baby pause and take breaks.
  • Burp often: Burp halfway through and at the end of each feed.
  • Check nipple flow: Too fast can cause gulping; too slow can cause frustration and air swallowing.
  • Warm consistently: A bottle warmer for night feedings helps keep feeds smooth at 3 AM.
  • Soothe after feeds: A snug wrap can calm a fussy baby; see our best swaddle for newborns.

Frequently asked questions

Do anti-colic bottles really work?

Anti-colic bottles are designed to reduce the air babies swallow, which can help with gas, spit-up and fussiness. Dr. Brown's and MAM say their vent systems are clinically proven to reduce colic, but results vary and colic can have other causes, so talk to your pediatrician if crying is frequent.

Which anti-colic bottle is easiest to clean?

Bottles with fewer parts are easier. Philips Avent says its one-piece AirFree vent is easy to clean without special tools, and the Nanobebe Flexy and Tommee Tippee Natural Start have simple designs. Dr. Brown's internal vent adds parts but can be removed as feeding develops.

What nipple flow should a newborn use?

Most newborns start on a slow or level 1 flow. The Dr. Brown's 4-pack comes with Level 1 slow flow nipples, and the MAM Easy Start includes an extra slow nipple for the tiniest babies. Move up a flow when feeds take too long or your baby seems frustrated.

How many baby bottles do I need?

Many families keep six to eight bottles for a newborn who is exclusively bottle fed, and fewer if bottles are occasional. Try one or two of a style first, since babies can be picky about nipples.

Can anti-colic bottles help with reflux?

Some are designed with reflux in mind. Philips Avent says its AirFree vent keeps the nipple full even when held horizontally, allowing upright feeding that can help reduce reflux. Always discuss reflux symptoms with your pediatrician.
