How to choose a baby gate for stairs

  1. Top or bottom: The top of stairs needs a hardware-mounted gate. Pressure mounting is only for the bottom of stairs, doorways and hallways.
  2. Measure carefully: Measure the opening where the gate will sit, wall to wall or wall to banister, and check the gate's width range.
  3. Banisters: Round railings need banister kits or Y-bolts to mount securely.
  4. Swing direction: At the top of the stairs, the gate must never swing out over the steps.
  5. Threshold: A bottom bar can be a trip hazard on stairs. Hardware-mounted gates often have no threshold.
  6. Locks: Double locks and auto-close doors add security, as long as adults can still open them easily.
  7. Certification: Look for Baby Safety Alliance or ASTM certification in the listing.

Stair gate safety tips

  • Hardware at the top, always: Never use a pressure-mounted gate at the top of the stairs.
  • No swing over the steps: Set the gate to open toward the landing, not over the stairs.
  • Mount into solid material: Screw into studs or solid wood, or use a banister kit, not just drywall.
  • Check monthly: Tighten pressure mounts and screws regularly, since they loosen with daily use.
  • Never climb over: Walking through instead of stepping over keeps adults from tripping.
  • Remove when outgrown: Once a child can climb or open the gate, it can become a hazard.
  • Babyproof the rest: Gates work best alongside other safety steps, like anchoring furniture and choosing a stable high chair for small spaces.

Pressure-mounted vs hardware-mounted gates

Pressure-mounted gates press against both sides of an opening using tension, so they install without tools and leave no holes, which is ideal for renters, doorways and the bottom of stairs. Hardware-mounted gates screw into the wall, door frame or banister, take longer to install and leave holes, but they cannot be pushed out of place and usually have no bottom bar. That makes them the only safe choice at the top of the stairs. Gates like the Cumbor and InnoTruth give you both options in one box.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of baby gate is safe for the top of stairs?

Use a hardware-mounted gate that screws into the wall or banister. Regalo warns never to use its pressure-mounted gate at the top of stairs, while the Toddleroo gate is hardware-mounted and designed for the top or bottom of a staircase.

Can you use a pressure-mounted baby gate on stairs?

Only at the bottom of stairs. Pressure-mounted gates can be pushed out of place, so they are not safe at the top where a fall is possible. For the top, choose hardware mounting.

How do I install a baby gate on stairs with banisters?

Look for a gate with banister hardware. Cumbor and InnoTruth include Y-shaped bolts that fit round stair railings, and Cumbor says this allows secure installation with no drilling.

How wide a baby gate do I need?

Measure the opening at the height where the gate will mount, then choose a gate whose range covers it. Regalo's extra wide gate only fits certain widths depending on which extensions you use, so check its fit chart carefully.

At what age do you stop using a baby gate?

Follow the gate maker's guidance. Regalo lists its gate for babies 6 to 24 months, and most families remove gates once a child can climb over or open them, often around age 2.
