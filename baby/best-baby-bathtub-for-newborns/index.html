What to look for in a newborn bathtub

  1. Newborn support: A sling, insert or cushion that cradles the head and keeps baby reclined.
  2. Grow-with-baby design: Many tubs convert to a sitting or toddler tub, so you buy once.
  3. Where you bathe: Sink bathers suit small homes; full tubs sit in an adult tub or on a counter.
  4. Storage: Collapsible tubs fold flat, and many tubs have a hook for drying.
  5. Drainage: A drain plug lets you empty the tub without lifting it full of water.

Bath time tips for newborns

  • Gather everything first: Towel, washcloth, baby wash, clean diaper and clothes should be within reach before you fill the tub.
  • Use only a few inches of water: Newborns need very little water.
  • Check the temperature: Test with your wrist or elbow, or use a thermometer.
  • Keep the room warm: Babies lose heat quickly when wet, so warm the bathroom and wrap them in a hooded towel right away.
  • Keep it short: A few minutes is plenty for a newborn.
  • Make bedtime easy: A warm bath can become part of a calming routine; see our winter baby essentials.

Frequently asked questions

When can I start bathing my newborn in a baby tub?

Many pediatricians suggest sponge baths until the umbilical cord stump falls off, then moving to a baby tub. Ask your pediatrician what is right for your baby.

What type of baby bathtub is best for a newborn?

Look for a tub with a newborn sling, insert or cushion that supports the head and keeps the baby reclined. The Fisher-Price, The First Years and Skip Hop picks all include newborn support, and the collapsible MoreFeel and TPN tubs include soft cushions.

How warm should baby bath water be?

Bath water should feel warm, not hot. Many guides suggest about 100°F, and you should always test it with your wrist or elbow. The MoreFeel and TPN tubs include temperature sensors to help.

Are collapsible baby bathtubs safe?

Collapsible tubs are popular for small homes and travel. Use them on a flat, stable surface, make sure they are fully opened and locked, and never leave your baby unattended, even for a moment.

Can I use a baby tub in the kitchen sink?

Yes, some are designed for it. The Skip Hop Soft Sink bather fits bathroom and kitchen sinks, and Fisher-Price says its tub fits most sinks and adult bathtubs. Move the faucet away and check the water temperature first.
