How to choose a gift for a 1 year old

  • Follow their stage, not their age. A child who is just pulling up will love a push toy; a confident walker may be ready for a balance bike.
  • Read the age label. Every toy here lists a recommended age range. Stick to it, especially for toys with small parts.
  • Mix noisy and quiet. Balance an electronic toy with a simple one like stacking cups so playtime is not all beeps and songs.
  • Ask the parents first. Many families have limited space or a preference for quieter toys, and they will appreciate the check.
  • Buy batteries. Electronic toys often ship with demo batteries only. Wrap a pack of AAs with the gift.
  • Order early. Popular toddler toys can sell out close to Christmas, so check stock and delivery dates.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good Christmas gift for a 1 year old?

Choose a toy that matches what they are learning right now: pushing and walking, stacking, simple pretend play or first words. A push toy like the VTech Pop-a-Balls Bulldozer or a set of stacking cups gets played with long after Christmas morning.

Is a balance bike safe for a 1 year old?

The SEREED baby balance bike is designed for 12 to 24 months, with no pedals, closed wheels and a 135-degree turning limit according to the listing. As with any ride-on toy, an adult should always supervise.

Can a 1 year old use the LeapFrog 100 Words Book?

LeapFrog lists the 100 Words Book for ages 18 months and up, so it is best as a gift a 1 year old will grow into over the next few months, with a grown-up reading along.

How many toys should a 1 year old get for Christmas?

One or two thoughtful toys are plenty. Toddlers this age often love the box as much as the gift, and fewer toys make it easier for them to focus and play longer.

The LeapFrog Picnic Basket needs 3 AA batteries and the 100 Words Book needs 2 AA batteries; both include batteries for demo purposes only. The VTech bulldozer is electronic too, so buy fresh batteries. The stacking cups and balance bike need none.
