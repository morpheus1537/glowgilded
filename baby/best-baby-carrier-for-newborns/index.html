How to choose a baby carrier for a newborn

  1. Minimum weight: Check that the carrier is rated for your baby's weight from day one.
  2. Ergonomic seat: Look for a seat that supports the M position, knees above hips.
  3. Head support: Newborns need head and neck support.
  4. Your comfort: Padded shoulder straps and a waist belt spread weight.
  5. Ease of use: Fewer buckles and simple adjustments help when you are tired.
  6. Longevity: Some carriers last into toddlerhood; others focus on the first year.

Baby carrier safety tips

  • Follow T.I.C.K.S.: Tight, In view at all times, Close enough to kiss, Keep chin off the chest, Supported back.
  • Check the weight range: Use the carrier only within its listed limits.
  • Face newborns in: Wait for good head control before facing out.
  • Practice first: Try the carrier with a stuffed animal or a helper nearby.
  • Watch the temperature: Babywearing adds warmth, so dress baby in one fewer layer.
  • Inspect buckles and seams: Check them regularly for wear.

Frequently asked questions

When can you start using a baby carrier with a newborn?

Many carriers are designed for newborns from about 7 or 8 lbs. Ergobaby Embrace is made for baby's first 12 months, Momcozy PureHug starts at 7 lbs and Infantino Flip and the Momcozy wrap start at 8 lbs. Always follow the minimum weight on the label.

What is the safest carrying position for a newborn?

Newborns should be carried facing in, upright and high enough to kiss the top of their head, with their face visible and airway clear. Their legs should be in an ergonomic M position with knees higher than hips.

Is a wrap or a structured carrier better for a newborn?

Wraps like the Momcozy wrap are soft and snug, which many parents like in the early weeks. Structured carriers like the Infantino Flip add buckles and padded straps and often last longer as baby grows.

Are baby carriers good for your back?

A well-fitted carrier spreads your baby's weight across your shoulders and hips. Look for padded straps and a supportive waist belt, like the EVA waist support on the Momcozy PureHug.

Can baby face out in a newborn carrier?

Facing out is usually for older babies with good head control. Ergobaby Embrace and Infantino Flip offer outward-facing carry, but newborns should face in.
