Types of ergonomic mice

  1. Vertical mice: Hold your hand in a handshake position. Good for reducing forearm twist.
  2. Thumb trackballs: Your hand stays still while your thumb moves the ball. Good for small desks.
  3. Contoured mice: A sculpted shape with a natural tilt, but closer to a traditional mouse.
  4. Hand size: Ergonomic mice are often sized; the wrong size can be less comfortable than a basic mouse.

Tips for a more comfortable mouse setup

  • Keep the mouse close: Place it right beside the keyboard so you do not reach.
  • Elbow at about 90 degrees: Adjust chair or desk height so your forearm is level.
  • Tune cursor speed: Higher DPI means less hand movement, but set it so you stay in control.
  • Take breaks: Short stretch breaks help no matter what mouse you use.
  • Free up space: A monitor arm for a small desk gives you more room for your mouse and keyboard.

Frequently asked questions

Is a vertical mouse better for wrist pain?

Many people find a vertical mouse more comfortable because it holds the hand in a handshake position instead of flat on the desk. Logitech says its MX Vertical's 57° angle reduces wrist pressure. Comfort varies by person, and persistent pain should be checked by a doctor.

How long does it take to get used to a vertical mouse?

Expect a short adjustment period. ProtoArc suggests giving its vertical mouse one to two weeks, as the new grip can feel unusual at first.

What size ergonomic mouse do I need?

Measure your hand from wrist crease to fingertip. ProtoArc designs the EM11 NL for hands under 7.5 inches, and Logitech says the Lift is a great fit for small to medium right hands. The MX Vertical is the larger Logitech vertical option.

Are trackball mice good for wrist pain?

A thumb trackball like the Logitech ERGO M575S lets you move the cursor with your thumb while your hand stays still, which some people find more comfortable. Logitech says it is a good fit for most hand sizes.

Are there left-handed ergonomic mice?

The models in this guide are designed for right hands. Some brands sell left-handed vertical mice separately, so check the listing before you buy.
