How to set up an ergonomic home office

  • Top of the screen at eye level: your eyes should land near the top third of the screen while you sit tall. A laptop stand or monitor riser gets you there.
  • Elbows at about 90 degrees: keep the keyboard close and low enough that your shoulders stay relaxed.
  • Light the desk, not the screen: place light so it does not reflect off the monitor. A light bar or a lamp to the side works best.
  • Feet flat: adjust chair height so your feet rest on the floor, or add a footrest.
  • Move often: stand up, stretch and look away from the screen every 30 to 60 minutes.

Small upgrades that add up

After these four basics, the next improvements are a supportive chair, simple cable management (adhesive clips or a cable tray), and a dedicated spot for a phone and notebook. Keep the desk surface as clear as possible. A tidy desk is easier to clean and easier to focus at, and it looks better on camera.

Frequently asked questions

What do I need for a basic home office setup?

A desk and supportive chair, a way to put your screen at eye level (a laptop stand or monitor), a separate keyboard and mouse, good lighting, and a reliable internet connection. A desk pad and simple cable management finish the setup.

Do I need an external keyboard with a laptop stand?

Yes. Once the laptop is raised to eye level, its built-in keyboard is too high to type on comfortably, so pair the stand with a wireless keyboard and mouse such as the Logitech MK335.

Will a monitor light bar cause glare on my screen?
