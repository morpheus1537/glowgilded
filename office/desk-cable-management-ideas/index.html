How to manage desk cables in 30 minutes

  1. Unplug and sort. Pull everything out, decide what stays on the desk, and remove cables you no longer use.
  2. Mount the tray. Clamp the tray under the back of the desk and put the power strip, USB hub and power bricks in it.
  3. Plug in from above. Plug each device into the strip in the tray, keeping cords short.
  4. Bundle the slack. Fold extra length and strap it with reusable ties inside the tray.
  5. One line to the wall. Run the remaining cords down a desk leg inside the split sleeve.
  6. Clip the daily cables. Add a few adhesive clips at the back edge for phone and laptop chargers.

Buying tips

  • Measure first. Check your desk edge thickness for a clamp tray and your power strip size for a cable box.
  • Tray or box? Use a tray if you can lift the strip off the floor; use a box if it must stay on the floor.
  • Plan for change. Choose reusable ties over zip ties so adding a new device takes seconds.
  • Clean before you stick. Wipe surfaces before using adhesive clips so they hold.
  • Leave some slack. Standing desks need enough cord length to move up and down without pulling plugs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I hide cables on my desk without drilling?

Use a clamp-on under-desk tray for the power strip and chargers, then bundle the rest with reusable hook-and-loop ties and adhesive clips. The Litwaro tray clamps onto desk edges from 0.4 to 2.4 inches thick, so no screws are needed.

What is the easiest way to manage cables under a desk?

Get the power strip off the floor first. Once it sits in an under-desk tray, every plug is short and close to the desk, and only one power cord has to run down to the wall outlet.

Are cable management boxes safe for power strips?

The D-Line box is made from electrically safe ABS plastic, according to the listing, and is designed to hold popular-sized power strips. Check your strip's size against the box's 16.5 x 6.5 x 5.25 inch outside dimensions, and do not pack it so tightly that cords are crushed.

Velcro ties or zip ties for cable management?

For a desk, reusable hook-and-loop ties are usually the better choice. You will add and remove devices over time, and hook-and-loop straps reopen without cutting, while zip ties have to be snipped and replaced.

How do I stop cables from falling behind my desk?

Stick a few multi-cable clips along the back or top edge of the desk and park your charging cables in them. The SOULWIT clips have an opening tab, so you can lift a cable out to use it and drop it back in when you are done.
