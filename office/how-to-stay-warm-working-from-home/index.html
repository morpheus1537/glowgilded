8 tips to stay warm in your home office

  1. Heat the room you are in. A small space heater in a closed office uses far less energy than heating the whole house.
  2. Close the door. Keep the heat where you are.
  3. Block drafts. Close curtains on cold windows and put a draft stopper at the door.
  4. Move your desk. If you can, sit away from exterior walls and windows.
  5. Warm your hands directly. A heated desk pad or fingerless gloves fix cold fingers fast.
  6. Layer up. A warm base layer, a sweater and a throw blanket over your lap go a long way.
  7. Use a rug. Bare floors pull heat from your feet.
  8. Move every hour. A short walk or stretch gets your circulation going again.

Space heater safety basics

  • Plug heaters directly into a wall outlet, not an extension cord or power strip.
  • Keep at least three feet of clearance from papers, curtains and blankets.
  • Turn heaters off when you leave the room.
  • Choose models with tip-over and overheat protection, like the DREO and Amazon Basics heaters above.

Frequently asked questions

How can I stay warm working from home without heating the whole house?

Heat the person and the room you are in, not the whole house. A space heater for the office, a heated desk pad for your hands, fingerless gloves, warm layers and a closed door keep you comfortable while the thermostat stays lower elsewhere.

Is a space heater safe for a home office?

Choose one with tip-over and overheat protection and a safety certification. The DREO heater in this guide has a tip-over sensor, overheat protection, V0 flame-retardant materials and ETL certification, according to the listing. Keep heaters clear of papers and fabric and plug them directly into a wall outlet.

Do heated desk pads work?

A heated desk pad warms your hands and forearms as you type. The tulpock pad has 3 heat levels from 104°F to 122°F and turns off automatically after 4 hours, according to the listing.

Why are my hands always cold when I work at a computer?

Sitting still for long periods reduces circulation to the hands and feet, and desks are often near cold windows or exterior walls. Fingerless gloves, a heated desk pad and regular movement breaks help.

What temperature should a home office be?

Comfort varies, but many people work best in the high 60s to low 70s °F. The DREO thermostat can be set from 41°F to 95°F in 1-degree steps so you can find your own sweet spot.
