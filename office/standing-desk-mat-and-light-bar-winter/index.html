Winter home office comfort tips

  • Alternate sitting and standing: Change position every 30 to 60 minutes rather than standing all day.
  • Wear supportive footwear: Socks alone on a mat feel good at first, but supportive slippers or shoes help over longer sessions.
  • Light the desk, not the screen: Put your main light on the desk surface and keep bright lamps out of your screen's reflection.
  • Use warmer light at night: Many light bars let you shift to a warmer color temperature in the evening.
  • Keep cords tidy: Route the light bar's USB cable behind the monitor so it does not snag.

Buying tips

  • Check monitor thickness: Light bar clamps list a supported thickness range; measure the top of your monitor.
  • Check curvature: If your monitor is curved, confirm the light bar supports it.
  • Size the mat to your space: Measure the area in front of your desk.
  • Look at the backing: Rubber backings are durable, but not every mat is slip-resistant.
  • Check the current listing: Mat sizes and colors vary, and ratings can cover several sizes.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a mat for a standing desk?

If you stand on hard floors for long stretches, an anti-fatigue mat can make standing more comfortable. The ComfiLife and Gorilla Grip mats both use 3/4-inch thick foam designed to reduce discomfort on feet, knees, legs and back.

Are monitor light bars worth it?

They are worth it if your desk is dark in the evening or a desk lamp causes glare. Light bars sit on top of the monitor and use asymmetric optics to light the desk without reflecting on the screen, which saves desk space and can make long winter evenings at the computer more comfortable.

Do monitor light bars work with curved monitors?

Some do. The Quntis listing says its clamp fits most 17 to 32-inch flat, curved and irregular monitors, and the BenQ ScreenBar Pro lists support for curvature from 1000R to 1800R. Check your monitor's thickness against each listing.

How thick should a standing desk mat be?

Many popular mats are about 3/4 inch thick, which balances cushioning and stability. Both mats in this guide are 3/4 inch. Very thick mats can feel unstable for some people.

Can I use a kitchen anti-fatigue mat at a standing desk?

Yes. Both mats here are sold for kitchens and standing desks. Note that the Gorilla Grip listing warns its mat is not slip-resistant, so keep it on a dry floor and lay it flat before use.
