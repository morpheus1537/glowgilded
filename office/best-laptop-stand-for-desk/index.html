How to choose a laptop stand

  1. Height: Fixed stands are simple; adjustable ones let you fine-tune height and angle.
  2. Laptop size: Check the listed range, such as 10 to 15.6 inches or up to 17.3 inches.
  3. Weight capacity: Heavier laptops need a sturdier stand.
  4. Stability: A wider, heavier base resists wobble when you type on the laptop directly.
  5. Portability: Folding stands travel well; heavier bases stay put.
  6. Style of use: Open for using the laptop screen, vertical for a closed laptop docked to a monitor.

Laptop stand setup tips

  • Add a keyboard and mouse: Keep your wrists low and relaxed while the screen is high.
  • Set screen height: Aim for the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level.
  • Sit back: Keep the screen about an arm's length away.
  • Manage cables: Route chargers neatly behind the stand.
  • Consider a monitor arm: If you add a second screen, our best monitor arm for small desks guide can help.
  • Take breaks: Stand up and move every so often, even with a perfect setup.

Frequently asked questions

Is a laptop stand worth it?

For many people, yes. Raising the screen closer to eye level can make long work sessions more comfortable. Pair it with an external keyboard and mouse so your wrists stay low while the screen is high.

How high should a laptop stand be?

A common guideline is to have the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level. The BESIGN LS03 raises a laptop by about 6 inches, while the tounee adjusts from 2.1 to 18 inches.

Do I need a keyboard with a laptop stand?

Usually yes. When the laptop is raised, its built-in keyboard is too high for comfortable typing, so most people add a separate keyboard and mouse.

Will a laptop stand keep my laptop cooler?

Open or vented stands allow more airflow under the laptop. BESIGN, Lamicall and Amazon Basics all list ventilation designs to help prevent overheating.

What is a vertical laptop stand for?

It holds a closed laptop upright, like the UGREEN stand, to save desk space when you use an external monitor, keyboard and mouse.
