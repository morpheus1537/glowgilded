Types of ergonomic keyboards

  1. Curved split keyboards: Keys are split into left and right halves with a curve, so your hands sit wider and more naturally.
  2. One-piece split keyboards: A single board with separated key zones, often with stepped, curved key rows.
  3. Comfort keyboards: A standard layout with a built-in palm rest and cushioned keys, easier to switch to.
  4. Low-profile keyboards: Thin, laptop-like keyboards with a gentle angle, good for fast, quiet typing.

Tips for a comfortable typing setup

  • Keyboard at elbow height: Your forearms should be roughly level, with relaxed shoulders.
  • Keep wrists straight: Avoid bending your hands up or sideways while typing. A flat or negative tilt can help.
  • Rest palms, not wrists: Use a wrist rest during pauses, but avoid pressing down on it as you type.
  • Bring the mouse close: A compact keyboard or one without a number pad keeps your mouse nearer.
  • Raise your screen: A monitor arm for a small desk puts the screen at eye level and frees space for your keyboard.
  • Take breaks: Short stretch breaks every hour help no matter what keyboard you use.

Frequently asked questions

Is a split keyboard better for typing all day?

Many people find a split or curved keyboard more comfortable because it lets the hands and wrists sit at a more natural angle. Logitech says the ERGO K860's curved, split keyframe reduces muscle strain on the wrists and forearms. Comfort varies, so consider your typing habits.

How long does it take to get used to an ergonomic keyboard?

Expect a short learning curve with split layouts. Perixx says most users adapt to its split PERIBOARD-512 within 1 to 2 weeks.

Should a keyboard tilt toward me or away from me?

Many ergonomic guides recommend a flat or negative tilt, where the front edge is higher than the back, to keep the wrists straighter. The Logitech ERGO K860 has tilt legs for 0, -4 and -7 degrees.

Do I need a wrist rest with my keyboard?

A wrist rest can support your hands during pauses. Logitech says the K860's memory foam rest offers 54 percent more wrist support, and the Signature K650 has an integrated soft-touch palm rest. Try to rest your palms rather than pressing your wrists down while typing.

Can an ergonomic keyboard fix wrist pain?

A keyboard alone cannot treat a medical condition. It may make typing more comfortable as part of a good setup, but persistent pain should be checked by a doctor or physical therapist.
