How to choose a foot cream for cracked heels

  1. Exfoliating ingredients: Urea and salicylic acid soften and loosen thick, hardened skin. Higher percentages are stronger.
  2. Protective moisture: Ingredients like petrolatum and shea butter create a protective layer that locks in moisture.
  3. Texture: Ointments are thick and best at night. Non-greasy creams work during the day and under socks.
  4. Scent: Unscented formulas suit sensitive skin. Scented creams with essential oils feel more like a spa treat.
  5. Size: Small jars and tubes travel well. Larger sizes last longer if you treat both feet daily.
  6. Health conditions: If you have diabetes or very deep cracks, check with a doctor first.

How to fix cracked heels at home

  • Soak and soften: A 10-minute warm soak softens skin before you apply cream.
  • Exfoliate gently: Use a pumice stone or foot file on damp skin a few times a week. Never cut or shave calluses yourself.
  • Moisturize at night: Apply a generous layer at bedtime when your feet can rest.
  • Wear socks: Cotton socks over cream lock in moisture and protect your sheets.
  • Choose supportive shoes: Closed-back shoes reduce the pressure that splits heels.
  • Make it a ritual: Add foot care to an at-home spa night so you actually stick with it.

Urea strength explained

Urea is a natural moisturizing factor found in skin. At low levels it mainly hydrates, while at higher levels, like the 25% in Flexitol and Dr. Scholl's, it also breaks down and softens thick, hard skin. Kerasal pairs 10% urea with 5% salicylic acid, another exfoliant, plus petrolatum to seal everything in. Higher strengths work faster on rough heels but can sting on open cracks, so start slowly and use a gentler cream like O'Keeffe's or Gold Bond if your skin is sore.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best ingredient for cracked heels?

Urea is one of the most common. It moisturizes and gently exfoliates thick, hardened skin, which is why Flexitol and Dr. Scholl's use 25% urea and Kerasal combines 10% urea with 5% salicylic acid.

How do you heal cracked heels fast?

Apply a thick foot cream or ointment every night after bathing, then wear cotton socks to lock it in. Flexitol and Kerasal both say users can see visible results in 1 day, though deep cracks take longer.

Is foot cream different from regular lotion?

Yes. Foot creams are usually thicker and often contain exfoliating ingredients like urea or salicylic acid to handle the thicker skin on heels, while body lotions are lighter.

Should I use foot cream at night or in the morning?

Night is ideal for most people. O'Keeffe's recommends applying at bedtime and after bathing, and Flexitol says its non-greasy balm works day or night, with or without socks.

When should I see a doctor about cracked heels?

See a doctor or podiatrist if cracks are deep, bleeding, painful or show signs of infection, or if you have diabetes or circulation problems, before treating them at home.
