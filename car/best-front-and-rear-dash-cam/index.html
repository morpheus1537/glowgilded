How to choose a dash cam

  • Front and rear vs front only: A rear camera records rear-end collisions and parking bumps; front-only is simpler to install.
  • Sensor matters as much as resolution: Three of the four cameras here use Sony STARVIS 2 sensors, which are popular for low-light recording.
  • Plan for parking mode: If you want recording while parked, budget for a hardwire kit or professional installation.
  • Get a good memory card: Use a high-endurance microSD card designed for constant recording if yours is not included.
  • Mount it correctly: Place the camera behind the rearview mirror so it does not block your view, and route cables along the headliner.

Frequently asked questions

Is a front and rear dash cam worth it?

For many drivers, yes. Rear-end collisions are common, and a rear camera records what happens behind you as well as in front. Dual dash cams also help in parking lot incidents if your model supports parking mode.

Do dash cams record when the car is off?

Only with parking mode, and most models need a hardwire kit to power the camera while parked. The ROVE, REDTIGER and galphi listings all say a hardwire kit is required and not included for parking mode.

What resolution should a dash cam be?

Higher resolution and a good image sensor help capture license plates and signs. The VIOFO A229 Plus records 1440P on both cameras, while the ROVE R2-4K records 2.5K at the front and upscales it to 4K, and 1080P at the rear.

Do dash cams come with a memory card?

Some do. The ROVE includes a 128GB card, the REDTIGER F17 includes a 64GB card and the galphi includes a 32GB card, according to their listings. Check the VIOFO listing before buying, since we did not see a card mentioned in its features.

Are dash cams legal?

Dash cams are legal to use in most of the US, but rules on windshield mounting and recording audio vary by state. Mount the camera where it does not block your view, and check your state's rules.
