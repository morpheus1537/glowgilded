Holiday road trip tips for dogs

  • Practice short drives: Take a few short trips before the big drive so your dog gets used to the seat cover and belt.
  • Use a harness: Attach seat belts to a well-fitted harness, never a collar.
  • Plan stops: Stop every few hours for water, a bathroom break and a short walk.
  • Pack a dog bag: Bring food, water, a bowl, waste bags, a towel for wet paws and a familiar blanket.
  • Never leave your dog alone in the car: Cars get dangerously cold or hot quickly.
  • Keep ID current: Make sure tags and microchip details are up to date before you travel.

Buying tips

Frequently asked questions

What is the best dog car seat cover for long road trips?

For long drives, a hard-bottom seat cover is the most comfortable because it creates a flat, stable platform across the back seat. The URPOWER cover lists a 51 by 23.6-inch hard bottom that holds up to 400 lbs, with zippered side flaps and a mesh window.

Is a hard bottom dog seat cover worth it?

If your dog likes to lie down or move around in the car, yes. A hard bottom keeps the dog from slipping into the footwell and makes the space feel more like a bed. A soft hammock is simpler, lighter and easier to fold away.

Are dog seat belts safe?

A dog seat belt helps keep your dog from roaming the car and distracting you. Always clip it to a harness, not a collar, and adjust the length so your dog can sit or lie down but not reach the front seats. The Mighty Paw listing recommends pairing its tether with an approved vehicle harness.

Do dog seat covers fit all cars?

Most universal covers fit typical sedans and SUVs, but measure your back seat first. The URPOWER listing says it is not recommended for captain's chairs or bucket seats.

How do I clean a dog car seat cover?

Wipe waterproof covers with a damp cloth after each trip, and shake or vacuum loose fur. The URPOWER support panels are detachable for deeper cleaning, according to the listing.
