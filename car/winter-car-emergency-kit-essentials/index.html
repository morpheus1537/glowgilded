What else to keep in your car this winter

The four picks above cover the essentials. Round out your kit with a few things you probably already own:

  • Warm layers: a spare hat, gloves and a fleece or old coat.
  • Water and snacks: a few bottles of water and calorie-dense snacks such as granola bars.
  • A phone charger: a car charger, or use the NOCO's power bank.
  • Traction help: a bag of cat litter or sand for tires stuck on ice, and a small folding shovel.
  • Organization: a trunk organizer keeps it all in one place; see our car organization essentials.

Buying tips

  • Check engine size: jump starters are rated for engine size. The GB40 covers up to 6.0L gas and 3.0L diesel.
  • Charge before you need it: put a reminder on your calendar to top up the jump starter every few months.
  • Keep the kit where you can reach it: in a crash or a ditch, the trunk may be hard to open. Keep blankets and the jump starter in the cabin if you can.

Frequently asked questions

What should be in a winter car emergency kit?

At minimum: a way to start a dead battery (jump starter or jumper cables), a snow brush and ice scraper, a first aid kit, a flashlight, warning triangles, gloves, and emergency blankets. Add water, snacks and a phone charger before long trips.

Is a portable jump starter better than jumper cables?

A jump starter lets you start a dead battery on your own, without waiting for a second car. The NOCO Boost GB40 is rated for engines up to 6.0L gas and 3.0L diesel and has spark-proof and reverse polarity protection.

Do Mylar emergency blankets really keep you warm?

They are designed to reflect body heat back to you. The listing for this 4-pack says the blankets retain up to 90% of body heat, and each unfolds to 82 by 52 inches while folding down small.

Does the Everlit kit include jumper cables?

Yes. It includes 12-foot, 8-gauge copper-clad aluminum jumper cables, plus a digital tire inflator, a 108-piece first aid kit, a reflective warning triangle, a tow rope and more.

Can I leave a lithium jump starter in a cold car?

Check the manufacturer's temperature guidance in the manual, since lithium batteries can lose charge in extreme cold. Many drivers keep it charged and bring it inside during deep freezes.
