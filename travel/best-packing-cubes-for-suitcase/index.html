How to choose packing cubes

  1. Number and sizes: Mixed sizes handle everything from socks to jackets.
  2. Compression: A second zipper squeezes clothes flatter to save space.
  3. Fabric: Thicker or ripstop fabric resists tears; water-repellent fabric helps with spills.
  4. Visibility: Mesh tops show what is inside.
  5. Extras: Some sets add shoe bags, laundry bags or toiletry pouches.
  6. Suitcase fit: Check the listed suitcase sizes or cube dimensions.

Packing cube tips for holiday trips

  • Roll, then cube: Rolling soft clothes fits more into each cube and reduces wrinkles.
  • One cube per person: Color-code cubes for each family member.
  • Pack by outfit: Put a full holiday outfit in one cube for quick changes.
  • Keep a laundry cube: Use an empty cube or laundry bag for dirty clothes.
  • Leave space for gifts: Pack cubes snugly but leave room for presents on the way home.
  • Match your bag: A carry-on backpack for weekend trips pairs well with smaller cubes.

Frequently asked questions

Are packing cubes worth it?

For most travelers, yes. Cubes sort clothes by type or by person, make it easy to find things without unpacking, and keep a suitcase tidy during a multi-stop trip.

Do compression packing cubes save space?

They can. A second zipper squeezes the cube flatter after you close it. Thule lists its medium cube at 5.9 inches deep expanded and 1.25 inches compressed, for example.

How full should a compression cube be?

Do not overstuff it. BAGAIL suggests filling each compression cube no more than two-thirds full before closing the main zipper and then the compression zipper.

Can packing cubes go in carry-on luggage?

Yes. Smaller cubes fit carry-ons, backpacks and duffels. Thule says its small and medium set fits most carry-on luggage with room left over.

Mesh or solid packing cubes: which is better?

Mesh tops let you see what is inside at a glance and let clothes breathe. Semitransparent or solid panels, like Thule's, keep contents more discreet.
