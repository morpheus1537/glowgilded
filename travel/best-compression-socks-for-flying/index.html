How to choose compression socks for flying

  1. Compression level: Look for the mmHg rating. 15-20 mmHg is a common everyday and travel level, while 20-30 mmHg is firmer.
  2. Graduated design: Graduated socks apply the most pressure at the ankle and less higher up, which is what you want for long periods of sitting.
  3. Size by calf, not just shoe: Measure the widest part of your calf. A sock that is too tight can dig in, and one that is too loose does little.
  4. Length: Knee-high socks are the standard for travel and cover the whole calf.
  5. Fabric: Breathable nylon and spandex blends feel cooler on long flights and dry faster if you wash them in a hotel sink.
  6. Pack size: Multi-pair packs let you wear a fresh pair on the way home.

Tips for wearing compression socks on a flight

  • Put them on early: Pull them on in the morning before your legs have a chance to swell.
  • Smooth out wrinkles: Bunched fabric creates uneven pressure. Smooth the sock from ankle to knee.
  • Never fold the top band: Rolling the cuff down concentrates pressure in one place.
  • Move when you can: Walk the aisle, flex your ankles and do seated calf raises during long flights.
  • Stay hydrated: Bring a refillable bottle and drink water regularly. Our carry-on packing essentials list includes a few good ones.
  • Pack a spare pair: A fresh pair for the return flight makes a long trip more comfortable.
  • Ask your doctor first if you have circulation problems, diabetes or a history of blood clots.

Frequently asked questions

Should you wear compression socks on a long flight?

Many travelers wear them on long flights because sitting still for hours can leave feet and ankles swollen and legs feeling heavy. Graduated compression socks are designed to support blood flow in the lower legs. If you have a history of blood clots, circulation problems or diabetes, ask your doctor before you fly.

What compression level is best for flying?

For most healthy travelers, 15-20 mmHg is a common choice for flights and long days of sitting. Firmer 20-30 mmHg socks, like SB SOX, offer more pressure; check with a doctor if you are unsure which level suits you.

When should I put on compression socks for a flight?

Put them on before you leave for the airport, ideally in the morning before your legs swell. Iseasoo also notes that compression socks are much easier to put on first thing in the morning.

How do I choose the right size compression socks?

Measure your calf at its widest point and choose by both shoe size and calf measurement. Compression socks should feel snug, but if you are between sizes or new to compression, Iseasoo suggests sizing up.

Can I sleep in compression socks on an overnight flight?

Many people keep them on for the whole flight, including naps in their seat. If you have a medical condition or the socks feel painful, numb or too tight, take them off and talk to your doctor.
