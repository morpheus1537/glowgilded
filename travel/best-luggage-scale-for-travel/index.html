How to choose a luggage scale

  1. Capacity: Most digital scales here weigh up to 110 lbs (50 kg), well over typical checked bag limits.
  2. Power: Battery, battery-free pump or manual.
  3. Display: A large or backlit screen is easier to read in a dim hotel room.
  4. Hold or lock: Auto weight lock keeps the reading after you lower the bag.
  5. Grip and hook: A comfortable handle and a strong strap or hook make lifting easier.
  6. Size: Smaller scales tuck into a pocket for the return trip.

Tips to avoid overweight bag fees

  • Weigh at home and before flying home: Souvenirs and gifts add up fast.
  • Leave a buffer: Aim a pound or two under the limit in case scales differ.
  • Rebalance between bags: Move heavy items like shoes and toiletries to the lighter bag.
  • Wear your heaviest layers: Boots and coats on the plane save suitcase weight.
  • Use compression: Packing cubes keep weight evenly spread and easy to shift.

Frequently asked questions

Are luggage scales accurate?

Good digital luggage scales are generally accurate enough to keep you under airline limits. The battery-free scale lists plus or minus 50 grams of precision, and Luxebell lists readings in 0.1-pound steps. Leave a small buffer below your airline's limit to be safe.

What is the weight limit for checked bags?

Many airlines use 50 pounds (23 kg) for economy checked bags, but limits vary by airline, route and fare class. Check your airline's website before you pack.

How do you use a hanging luggage scale?

Loop the strap or hook through the suitcase handle, lift the scale until the bag hangs freely off the floor, hold it steady and read the weight. Scales with an auto lock, like the Longang, hold the reading so you can set the bag down first.

Can I bring a luggage scale in my carry-on?

Yes, small luggage scales are common carry-on items. The battery-free scale weighs about 100 grams and fits in a jacket pocket, so you can weigh bags again before flying home.

Do I need a digital or manual luggage scale?

Digital scales are easier to read and offer features like tare and backlit screens. A manual scale like the Samsonite never needs batteries, though its 80-pound limit is lower than the 110-pound digital scales here.
